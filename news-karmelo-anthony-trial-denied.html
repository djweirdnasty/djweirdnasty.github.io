Judge Denies Karmelo Anthony’s Request for New Trial in Austin Metcalf Stabbing Case

National | Crime

Published: Monday, August 24, 2026

A Texas judge has rejected Karmelo Anthony’s request for a new trial, leaving the 19-year-old’s murder conviction and 35-year prison sentence intact while his defense prepares to take the case to an appellate court.

Retired Kaufman County District Judge Michael Chitty denied Anthony’s motion Saturday, Aug. 22, following a closely watched two-day hearing that revisited several controversial issues surrounding his June murder conviction in the death of 17-year-old Austin Metcalf.

Anthony was convicted of murder in connection with the April 2025 stabbing at a high school track meet in Frisco, Texas. The jury subsequently sentenced him to 35 years in prison.

Chitty’s ruling was brief. The judge said he had reviewed the defense motion, the trial record, evidence, arguments, legal briefs and applicable case law before determining that the request for a new trial should be denied.

The decision does not end Anthony’s legal fight. His attorneys have said they intend to pursue an appeal before the Texas Fifth Court of Appeals in Dallas.

Defense Argued Anthony Was Denied a Full Defense

A central issue in the request for a new trial involved an informal, off-the-record agreement between prosecutors and Anthony’s original defense attorneys concerning potentially damaging character evidence.

According to testimony presented during the hearing, both sides had agreed to avoid introducing certain information about the past behavior of Anthony and Metcalf. The arrangement was intended to keep the trial focused on the confrontation that occurred beneath a team tent at the track meet rather than allowing the proceedings to become dominated by inflammatory allegations about either teenager.

Anthony’s new attorneys argued that the agreement became a significant problem when their client was preparing to testify.

Defense lawyers claimed Anthony ultimately decided not to take the witness stand because of concerns that prosecutors might not honor the agreement if he testified. They argued that the dispute effectively interfered with his constitutional right to present a complete defense and deprived the jury of Anthony’s own account of what happened.

Russell Wilson, an appellate attorney representing Anthony, maintained that the issues raised by the defense deserved further review.

Following Saturday’s ruling, Wilson said the legal team would continue its appeal and pursue every lawful option available to Anthony.

Prosecutors Dispute Defense Claims

Prosecutors strongly rejected the suggestion that the agreement undermined Anthony’s trial.

Collin County First Assistant District Attorney Bill Wirskye testified during the hearing that the arrangement was made to keep the case centered on the events at the track meet and prevent damaging character evidence from escalating an already highly contentious case.

Wirskye maintained that the agreement was understood by both sides and was honored.

Collin County District Attorney Greg Willis also defended the original proceedings following the judge’s decision.

Willis said prosecutors expected the motion to be denied and maintained that the jury’s guilty verdict remains valid. He also emphasized that Anthony retains the right to appeal.

The case had generated intense national attention, with social media discussions frequently framing the confrontation through the lens of race. Anthony is Black and Metcalf was white. Attorneys on both sides, however, told jurors during the original trial that race was not the reason for the confrontation.

What Happened at the Track Meet

The fatal confrontation occurred during a high school track meet in Frisco in April 2025.

Anthony and Metcalf were athletes associated with different schools participating in the event. According to testimony presented during the case, Anthony was sitting underneath a tent designated for Metcalf’s team.

Witnesses said Metcalf and others repeatedly asked Anthony to leave the area. The encounter escalated into a physical confrontation.

Prosecutors argued that Anthony was the aggressor and that his actions did not constitute lawful self-defense.

According to a police report cited in coverage of the case, Anthony reached into a bag during the confrontation and warned, “Touch me and see what happens.”

Witnesses said Metcalf subsequently pushed Anthony. Anthony then pulled a knife and stabbed Metcalf in the chest.

Metcalf, 17, died from his injuries.

Anthony’s defense maintained that he acted in self-defense, but jurors ultimately rejected that argument and found him guilty of murder after deliberating for approximately three hours.

Anthony did not testify during the trial.

Courtroom Access Also Became Part of the Retrial Fight

The defense raised another issue in seeking a new trial: the courtroom restrictions imposed by former trial judge John Roach.

Anthony’s attorneys argued that Roach’s restrictions limited public access to the proceedings and interfered with the transparency of the trial.

The defense also sought Roach’s removal from further involvement in the case, pointing to comments the judge made after the verdict.

Roach had defended his courtroom procedures and indicated after the conviction that he believed the jury reached the correct conclusion.

A separate judge ultimately ordered Roach removed from the case after determining that evidence presented at a recusal hearing created grounds for questioning his impartiality.

Michael Chitty was subsequently assigned to handle matters involving Anthony.

Hearing Revealed Evidence Jurors Never Heard

The two-day hearing also provided a closer look at evidence that was kept away from the jury during the original murder trial.

Attorneys for both the prosecution and the defense testified about the negotiations surrounding the informal character-evidence agreement. Some of the evidence discussed involved previous behavior and communications involving both Anthony and Metcalf.

The information was not presented to jurors during the trial, in keeping with the agreement between the parties.

The defense argued that the dispute over what prosecutors could ask Anthony if he testified became significant enough to influence his decision not to take the stand.

Prosecutors, however, characterized the situation differently, arguing that the defense had agreed to the arrangement and that it did not prevent Anthony from receiving a fair trial.

Appeal Is Next

With the motion for a new trial denied, Anthony’s attorneys are expected to shift their focus to the appellate process.

An appeal will give the defense another opportunity to challenge legal issues arising from the trial, including the circumstances surrounding Anthony’s decision not to testify, the character-evidence agreement and the courtroom-access restrictions.

For now, however, the June conviction remains in place and Anthony remains sentenced to 35 years in prison.

The case has continued to attract significant public attention more than a year after Metcalf’s death, fueled by disputes over self-defense, courtroom procedure, evidence and the intensely polarized reaction that followed the stabbing.

The latest ruling means Anthony has not secured a second trial, but his legal team has made clear that Saturday’s decision is not the final step in the case.
