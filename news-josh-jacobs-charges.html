Packers RB Josh Jacobs Faces Two Misdemeanor Charges After May Incident

Sports | NFL

Published: Thursday, August 27, 2026

Green Bay Packers running back Josh Jacobs is facing two misdemeanor criminal charges stemming from an alleged confrontation with his girlfriend at his Wisconsin home in May, a case that could now have consequences for his availability when the NFL regular season begins.

The Brown County District Attorney’s Office announced Thursday that Jacobs has been charged with misdemeanor battery and misdemeanor criminal damage to property. The charges come more than three months after Jacobs was arrested following a police response to a reported disturbance at a residence near Green Bay.

The decision to file charges followed a continued investigation by law enforcement and prosecutors.

Brown County District Attorney David L. Lasee said investigators had obtained additional evidence after the initial arrest, prompting prosecutors to take another look at the case.

“Our office sought to ensure a fair and complete review of available evidence,” Lasee said, adding that some of the evidence only recently became available because of ongoing forensic work.

Lasee also emphasized that the criminal complaint represents an accusation rather than a finding of wrongdoing. Jacobs remains presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.

What the complaint alleges

According to the criminal complaint, the dispute began after Jacobs’ girlfriend looked through his phone and allegedly discovered conversations with other women. The woman told police she became upset and threw Jacobs’ phone, although she said the device was not broken.

The complaint says Jacobs subsequently had possession of the woman’s phone. She reportedly told him he could look through it and asked him to arrange transportation for her to Chicago.

When she attempted to retrieve her phone, police allege that Jacobs refused to give it back and grabbed her arm. A physical struggle allegedly followed and continued for several minutes.

The two eventually left the residence separately, according to the complaint, with the woman following Jacobs as she attempted to recover her phone.

The pair later returned to the home, where the dispute allegedly continued.

The complaint says the woman told police she intended to contact authorities to retrieve her phone. She then obtained another phone from a duffle bag.

According to investigators, she attempted to leave through the garage when Jacobs allegedly grabbed her and threw her to the ground. Police said she struck her head during the incident.

Officers who responded reported seeing redness around her face and neck and said they could feel a bump on the back of her head. The woman reportedly told police that she was experiencing pain.

She also alleged that one of her phones had not been returned and that another had been damaged.

Investigators said surveillance cameras in the garage captured portions of the encounter. The complaint states that the footage supported elements of the woman’s account.

Charges are narrower than the initial arrest

Jacobs’ legal situation has changed considerably since his May arrest.

Police initially arrested him on multiple allegations, including a felony count involving strangulation and suffocation. The original arrest also included allegations involving battery, criminal damage to property, disorderly conduct and intimidation of a victim.

At the time, prosecutors did not immediately pursue criminal charges, citing insufficient evidence available to them then. However, the district attorney’s office indicated that the investigation would remain open because additional evidence was believed to exist.

Thursday’s filing ultimately resulted in two misdemeanor counts.

Both are Class A misdemeanors under Wisconsin law and, if Jacobs were convicted, each charge carries potential penalties of up to nine months in jail, a fine of as much as $10,000, or both, according to the criminal complaint.

Jacobs’ attorneys stressed the distinction between the allegations that led to his arrest and the charges prosecutors ultimately filed.

“The Brown County District Attorney has reviewed evidence and elected to file misdemeanor charges against Josh that do not include domestic violence,” attorneys David Z. Chesnoff, Richard A. Schonfeld and Clarence F. Duchac III said in a statement.

The attorneys said Jacobs intends to address the allegations through the court system rather than through public statements.

Packers and NFL monitoring the case

The charges create another layer of uncertainty for a Packers team preparing for the upcoming regular season.

Jacobs has remained with Green Bay throughout the offseason and has participated in team activities and training camp when healthy. He did miss time during training camp because of a groin injury.

The Packers said Thursday they were aware of the newly filed charges and would continue cooperating with the NFL.

The league is conducting its own review and could impose discipline independently of the criminal case.

NFL spokesman Brian McCarthy said the league has been monitoring developments while its investigation remains ongoing.

That means Jacobs’ football status could ultimately be determined separately from the outcome of his criminal proceedings.

A potentially significant impact on Green Bay’s offense

The timing of the case is particularly important for the Packers because Jacobs is expected to play a major role in the team’s offense.

Green Bay signed Jacobs to a four-year, $48 million contract in 2024 after his departure from the Las Vegas Raiders. During his first two seasons with the Packers, he established himself as one of the team’s most important offensive players.

Jacobs rushed for 1,329 yards and 15 touchdowns during the 2024 season and followed that performance with 929 rushing yards and 13 touchdowns the following year.

For his career, the 28-year-old running back has accumulated 7,803 rushing yards and 74 touchdowns. He also earned All-Pro recognition and led the NFL with 1,653 rushing yards during the 2022 season with Las Vegas.

Green Bay has other running backs capable of taking on additional work, but a Jacobs suspension would force the team to adjust its plans.

Third-year running back MarShawn Lloyd could move into the starting role if Jacobs is unavailable. Chris Brooks and Pierre Strong Jr. also provide additional depth.

Packers general manager Brian Gutekunst acknowledged that the organization could consider carrying an extra running back if Jacobs’ availability becomes an issue.

Green Bay can also use practice-squad elevations to supplement its running back group during the season, something Gutekunst said the organization has done previously.

Teammates have continued working with Jacobs

Jacobs has continued practicing and working with the team while the legal process has unfolded.

Quarterback Jordan Love previously acknowledged that the situation had been discussed internally but said players were allowing the legal process to proceed rather than speculating publicly.

Coach Matt LaFleur has also spoken positively about Jacobs’ approach to his work with the team.

The Packers now face a waiting period as the criminal case moves forward and the NFL determines whether league discipline will be imposed.

Jacobs is scheduled to make his initial court appearance Nov. 17.

Until then, the criminal allegations remain unresolved. The charges filed Thursday do not establish guilt, and the case will proceed through the Wisconsin court system while the NFL continues its separate review.

For Green Bay, the immediate question is whether Jacobs will be available when the Packers open their regular-season schedule Sept. 13 against the Minnesota Vikings.

The answer could depend not only on what happens in court, but also on how the NFL responds to the allegations.
