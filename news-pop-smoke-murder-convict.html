Pop Smoke Murder Convict Gets 4 More Years in Fentanyl Case

Music News

Published: Monday, August 17, 2026

A man convicted in the 2020 killing of rapper Pop Smoke has been handed another prison sentence after prosecutors accused him of possessing fentanyl-laced pills while being held at a Los Angeles County juvenile facility.

Deijae Jester, now 21, was sentenced to four years in state prison after entering a no-contest plea in connection with the drug case, according to authorities and court records.

Jester was 15 when Pop Smoke, born Bashar Barakah Jackson, was fatally shot during a home invasion at a Hollywood Hills residence in February 2020.

New Prison Sentence Stems From Juvenile Facility Drug Case

The latest case dates back to December 2025, when Jester was being held at Barry J. Nidorf Juvenile Hall in Los Angeles County.

Prosecutors alleged that Jester’s mother, Nickia Randall, tried to get drugs into the facility during a visit. Authorities said officers discovered a container holding 25 pills that were later identified as fentanyl-laced, along with a small amount of powder.

Investigators reportedly examined surveillance footage and recorded conversations as part of the investigation. Prosecutors said the evidence pointed to discussions about bringing contraband into the facility.

Randall has also been charged in connection with the alleged smuggling attempt and, according to authorities, has not entered a plea.

Jester Was One of Several People Linked to Pop Smoke’s Death

Jester was among four individuals who authorities said entered the Hollywood Hills home where Pop Smoke was staying.

Investigators later tied Jester to the fatal shooting. During the investigation, detectives obtained information that prosecutors said indicated Jester acknowledged shooting the rapper following a confrontation inside the residence.

Pop Smoke was only 20 years old at the time of his death and had quickly become one of the most recognizable young voices in Brooklyn drill.

His breakout records, including “Welcome to the Party” and “Dior,” helped establish him as an international rap star before his career was abruptly cut short.

Prosecutors Sought a Longer Sentence

During the latest sentencing, prosecutors pushed for five years in prison, citing the dangers associated with fentanyl and the fact that the alleged drug offense occurred while Jester was already in custody for the homicide case.

Jester’s defense argued for leniency, pointing to his age when Pop Smoke was killed, his difficult upbringing and his efforts to pursue education and rehabilitation while incarcerated.

The judge ultimately sentenced him to four years.

The new punishment will be served in state prison, meaning Jester is expected to leave the juvenile detention system.

Other Defendants Faced Different Outcomes

Jester was not the only person prosecuted following Pop Smoke’s death.

Corey Walker, an adult defendant in the case, was sentenced to 29 years in prison for his role in the killing.

Other individuals connected to the case were juveniles when the crime occurred and were therefore processed through California’s juvenile justice system. Their sentences and custody periods differed based on their ages and individual circumstances.

Pop Smoke’s Influence Continues

More than six years after his death, Pop Smoke’s influence remains firmly planted in hip-hop.

The Brooklyn rapper’s posthumous debut album, Shoot for the Stars, Aim for the Moon, became a major commercial success and further cemented his status as one of drill music’s most influential young artists.

His death remains a defining tragedy in modern rap, while the criminal cases surrounding those responsible continue to produce new developments.

For Jester, the latest four-year sentence adds additional time behind bars years after his conviction in the case that followed the death of Pop Smoke.
