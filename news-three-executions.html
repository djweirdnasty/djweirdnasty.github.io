Three Death Row Inmates Set to Die in Rare Same-Day Execution Across 3 States

National | Death Penalty

Published: Wednesday, August 13, 2026

Three death-row prisoners in Tennessee, Alabama and Oklahoma are scheduled to be executed Thursday, creating an unusually rare day in which three lethal injections are set to take place across the United States.

The men are facing execution in three separate cases. Tennessee plans to put Anthony Darrell Hines to death for the 1985 killing of motel worker Catherine Jean Jenkins. In Oklahoma, 70-year-old Carlos Cuesta-Rodriguez is scheduled to be executed for the 2003 shooting death of his girlfriend, Olimpia Fisher. Alabama is preparing to execute Jeremy Williams for the 2021 rape and murder of 5-year-old Kamarie Holland.

If all three executions proceed, six states will have carried out executions in the United States during 2026. Eleven states conducted executions during 2025, with Florida accounting for the largest number at 19.

A three-execution day is highly unusual. The last time three prisoners were actually executed on the same day was Jan. 7, 2010, when inmates were put to death in Louisiana, Ohio and Texas. In 2018, three executions were scheduled for one day, but only one ultimately occurred after one prisoner received a reprieve and Alabama officials encountered problems establishing an IV.

Tennessee: Anthony Darrell Hines

Hines, now 66, was convicted of killing Jenkins while she was working as a motel maid in Kingston Springs, Tennessee. He stabbed the 54-year-old woman in 1985 and was initially sentenced to death the following year. After winning an appeal that resulted in a new sentencing hearing, he received another death sentence in 1989.

His scheduled execution has drawn additional attention because Tennessee recently experienced complications during another lethal injection. In May, officials stopped the execution of Tony Carruthers after struggling to establish a second IV line. The procedure reportedly continued for more than an hour before Gov. Bill Lee halted it and granted Carruthers a one-year reprieve.

Hines’ attorneys argued that similar problems could occur during his execution, citing his physical condition and previous strokes. They also questioned the qualifications of the doctor involved in Carruthers’ attempted execution. Tennessee courts rejected those arguments, concluding that Hines had not demonstrated that the doctor was unqualified.

Alabama: Jeremy Williams

In Alabama, Williams has chosen not to pursue further appeals and has indicated that he wants his death sentence carried out. He pleaded guilty in the killing of Kamarie Holland, a 5-year-old Georgia girl.

Prosecutors said Williams paid the child’s mother $2,500 in exchange for access to the girl, then sexually assaulted and strangled her. Authorities also said he recorded the abuse and continued abusing the child’s body after her death.

Kamarie’s father, Corey Holland, remembered his daughter as a joyful child who enjoyed playing dress-up and wearing princess costumes. Her mother pleaded guilty to sex trafficking and received a 20-year prison sentence.

If carried out, Williams’ execution would be Alabama’s first of 2026. The state recently faced a legal setback involving its use of nitrogen hypoxia after a federal judge ruled against the method in June.

Oklahoma: Carlos Cuesta-Rodriguez

In Oklahoma, Cuesta-Rodriguez is scheduled to receive a lethal injection at the Oklahoma State Penitentiary in McAlester. He was convicted of killing Fisher at their Oklahoma City home in 2003.

Authorities said Fisher was preparing to leave the relationship when Cuesta-Rodriguez shot her. Prosecutors argued that the killing was an act of control and domestic violence.

Cuesta-Rodriguez has expressed remorse for the killing but did not seek clemency from Oklahoma’s Pardon and Parole Board. His attorneys argued that his troubled childhood in Cuba and reported brain damage, post-traumatic stress disorder and severe depression should be considered in determining whether his death sentence should be carried out.

The Oklahoma board voted 3-1 against recommending clemency last month, leaving the execution on schedule.

Cuesta-Rodriguez would become the third person executed in Oklahoma this year and the 20th executed during Gov. Kevin Stitt’s tenure if the state carries out the sentence.

The simultaneous scheduling of the three executions highlights the continued use of capital punishment in the United States, while also drawing attention to the legal challenges, appeals and procedural complications that can determine whether an execution actually proceeds as planned.
