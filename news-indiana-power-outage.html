Northwest Indiana Battles Prolonged Blackouts as Crews Work Through Massive Storm Damage

National | Weather

Published: Tuesday, August 18, 2026

A week after a powerful storm system battered Northwest Indiana, thousands of residents are still waiting for their electricity to return, with some communities facing several more days of outages.

The widespread damage has left utility crews working around the clock while residents deal with fallen trees, damaged power lines, debris-filled streets and, in some neighborhoods, homes that have been without electricity for days.

Munster and Highland Facing the Longest Wait

NIPSCO’s latest restoration projections show that some customers in Munster and Highland may not have their electricity restored until late Tuesday, August 25.

The utility stresses that the dates represent estimates for when restoration work is expected to be substantially completed throughout each community. Individual homes may be restored sooner or, in some cases, later.

Other communities have earlier targets, with restoration work continuing throughout the region.

More Than 90,000 Customers Still Without Power

The scale of the damage has forced NIPSCO to bring in a large number of workers and contractors.

The utility reported Tuesday night that approximately 92,000 customers remained without power, while about 278,000 customers had been restored since the latest storm event.

NIPSCO said the August 11 storm alone generated roughly 301,000 outages, affecting more than 60% of its customer base.

The utility’s infrastructure suffered significant damage, including more than 1,500 poles, five transmission towers and approximately 2.1 million feet of electrical wire.

About 1,440 line workers from NIPSCO and contractor organizations are involved in the restoration effort, along with hundreds of crews focused on vegetation and debris removal.

Portage Still Recovering

Portage was among the communities hit especially hard.

Mayor Austin Bonta said the city initially had approximately 15,000 homes without electricity. By Tuesday, power had returned to about half of the city’s residents, although numerous customers remained without service.

Much of the remaining work is concentrated in areas where electrical equipment and fallen trees are intertwined.

Local workers have cleared the majority of major roads, but officials say some locations cannot safely be opened until utility crews deal with damaged power infrastructure.

The city is also expanding its capacity to handle storm debris as existing disposal areas approach capacity.

Frustration Growing Among Residents

For people who have spent nearly a week without electricity, patience is becoming increasingly difficult.

Some residents have turned to generators, campers and community assistance centers for basic necessities. Others have had to travel outside their communities for fuel, food and places with air conditioning.

In some areas, residents have also questioned how quickly crews are reaching individual neighborhoods and expressed frustration over limited information about restoration schedules.

Local officials acknowledge the enormous scale of the storm while continuing to press for progress.

Eight Tornadoes Confirmed

The damage wasn’t caused by a single type of storm.

The National Weather Service said a derecho moved through the region, generating widespread straight-line winds that reached extremely high speeds.

A wind gust of 99 mph was recorded at Gary/Chicago International Airport.

Eight tornadoes were also confirmed within the National Weather Service’s Chicago forecast area. The tornadoes ranged from EF-0 to EF-2, with one EF-2 track extending from the Monee area toward Crown Point.

The combination of tornadoes and powerful straight-line winds damaged trees, homes, roads and utility infrastructure across parts of Illinois and Indiana.

Another Round of Storms Could Arrive

The recovery effort could face another challenge Tuesday night into Wednesday.

Forecasters warned of thunderstorms, with heavy rainfall and potentially damaging winds among the primary threats. Even storms that don’t produce tornadoes could complicate cleanup and restoration operations.

Residents dealing with damaged properties and extended outages are being encouraged to remain alert as crews continue their work.

Safety Remains a Major Concern

Officials are warning residents not to approach downed electrical lines.

A wire lying on the ground can still be energized, even if there are no visible sparks or other signs of electricity. Trees, fences, vehicles and standing water near a fallen line can also pose serious dangers.

NIPSCO’s restoration operation is expected to continue around the clock as crews work through damaged neighborhoods.

For many residents, however, the biggest question remains simple: When will the lights finally come back on?

For customers in the hardest-hit communities, the answer could still be several days away.
