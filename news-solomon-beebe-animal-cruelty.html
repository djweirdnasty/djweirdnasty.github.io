Virginia Running Back Solomon Beebe Faces Animal Cruelty Charges Ahead of Season Opener

Sports | College Football

Published: Friday, August 21, 2026

University of Virginia running back and kick returner Solomon Beebe is facing three misdemeanor animal-cruelty charges after authorities investigated allegations involving his pet pit bull at his apartment complex in Albemarle County.

Beebe, who transferred to Virginia after two seasons at the University of Alabama at Birmingham, was arrested Monday and appeared in Albemarle County District Court on Thursday. According to court records cited by multiple outlets, the charges involve three alleged incidents reported on Aug. 10 and Aug. 12.

Authorities seized Beebe’s dog on Aug. 13 and placed the animal in protective custody at the Albemarle County SPCA while the investigation proceeded.

Witness Reported Multiple Alleged Encounters

The allegations came to light after a housekeeper at the apartment complex where Beebe lives north of Charlottesville reportedly told animal-control investigators that she witnessed Beebe striking his dog.

According to investigative records reported by The Daily Progress, the housekeeper described an incident on Aug. 12 in which Beebe allegedly held the dog against a wall and struck it with a closed fist multiple times. The witness reportedly said the animal was yelping during the encounter.

The same witness also described another alleged confrontation later that day, saying Beebe held the dog down in a hallway and struck it across the face.

Animal-control officials also investigated an earlier allegation dating to Aug. 10, which is reflected in the three charges filed against Beebe.

The allegations remain just that—allegations—and Beebe has not been convicted of animal cruelty.

Dog Placed in Protective Custody

Following the investigation, Albemarle County animal-control officers took custody of Beebe’s dog on Aug. 13.

Assistant Commonwealth’s Attorney Shannon Pollock reportedly sought continued custody of the animal, with officials saying the dog was being held at the local SPCA as the case moved through the court system.

Beebe was released following his arrest on an unsecured bond. His next scheduled court appearance is Oct. 1, according to CBS19 News.

His attorney, Thomas Weidner, was reportedly unavailable for immediate comment when contacted by Fox News.

Virginia Declines to Discuss Pending Case

The allegations come at an important point in Beebe’s football career.

Beebe joined Virginia after entering the transfer portal in December following two seasons at UAB. The Montgomery, Alabama, native had emerged as a versatile contributor for the Blazers, contributing as both a running back and kick returner.

Virginia confirmed that it was aware of the situation but declined to discuss the pending criminal matter.

“The University does not comment on matters involving ongoing criminal proceedings or student records,” the school said in a statement.

The Cavaliers have not publicly announced a decision regarding Beebe’s status with the football program.

Beebe Was Competing for a Role on Virginia’s Offense

Before the allegations surfaced, Beebe had been working his way into Virginia’s plans for the upcoming season.

During his final season at UAB in 2025, Beebe rushed for 338 yards and six touchdowns on 56 carries. He also recorded 284 receiving yards on 31 catches and contributed 600 yards on kickoff returns.

His ability to contribute in multiple phases made him a potentially valuable piece of Virginia’s roster. He had been working with the Cavaliers’ running back group while also competing for opportunities in the return game.

Virginia head coach Tony Elliott recently praised Beebe’s ability after the transfer joined the program.

Elliott said Beebe quickly demonstrated that he could be an effective running back and had earned a place in the team’s rotation. The coach also pointed to Beebe’s potential as a return specialist.

However, Elliott did not speak with reporters Thursday following news of the arrest, leaving questions about Beebe’s immediate future with the team unanswered.

Cavaliers Prepare for Season Opener

The timing of the legal situation is particularly significant for Virginia because the Cavaliers are less than two weeks away from opening their 2026 season.

Virginia is scheduled to host NC State on Aug. 29 in an ACC matchup.

If Beebe is unavailable, Virginia could turn to Tennessee transfer Peyton Lewis and MTSU transfer Jekail Middlebrook for additional responsibilities in the backfield. Beebe’s absence could also affect the team’s plans for kickoff returns.

For now, however, the football implications remain secondary to the criminal case and the ongoing investigation into the allegations.

Beebe is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in court. The three charges are misdemeanor offenses, and the case remains pending in Albemarle County.

As the legal proceedings continue, Virginia will have to determine how the allegations affect Beebe’s participation in team activities and whether he will be available when the Cavaliers take the field against NC State on Aug. 29.
