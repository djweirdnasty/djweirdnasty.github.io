Concord Mall Could Face Major Demolition and Redevelopment

National | Delaware

Published: Friday, August 14, 2026

WILMINGTON, Del. — Concord Mall, a longtime shopping destination in northern Delaware, could soon look dramatically different as officials review plans that would remove most of the aging enclosed shopping center and replace it with a much smaller retail development.

A redevelopment application submitted to New Castle County proposes demolishing approximately 846,896 square feet of the existing mall. The plan calls for a new, roughly 233,100-square-foot, one-story retail building, cutting the property’s enclosed retail space by more than 70%.

The proposal would leave Boscov’s in place, while the former Sears property is also identified as remaining. However, the Sears building has separate ownership, making its long-term future less certain.

Several businesses located outside the main mall complex, including T-Mobile, Best Buy, Sprouts and Ulta, are not included in the proposed demolition.

Concord Mall opened in 1969 and once attracted shoppers from throughout Delaware and southeastern Pennsylvania. During its strongest years, the mall housed more than 90 retailers and was nearly fully occupied.

Today, the property has experienced a steep decline in traditional retail traffic. Only a small number of stores remain inside, while several recognizable brands have recently closed or moved out. Macy’s remains, but its home-goods store has also shut down.

The mall has undergone several attempts at reinvention over the years. Previous redevelopment ideas included creating a mixed-use destination, but those efforts did not ultimately move forward.

Earlier this year, Abrams Realty and Development announced plans involving the property that could eventually include housing, a fitness facility, grocery shopping and green space. The company has previously used a similar strategy at another aging mall while retaining Boscov’s.

For longtime shoppers, the potential changes are about more than buildings and businesses. Concord Mall was once a central part of the community, and its decline represents the changing nature of retail as consumers increasingly shop online and traditional department stores disappear.

The current demolition proposal still has to go through New Castle County’s review process, including consideration of site plans and traffic impacts. Public hearings could also be required. Until those reviews are completed, there is no guarantee that the project will proceed exactly as currently proposed.

If approved, however, the redevelopment would mark a dramatic change for one of northern Delaware’s most recognizable shopping centers — replacing the large enclosed mall of the past with a significantly smaller retail footprint designed for a different era of shopping.
