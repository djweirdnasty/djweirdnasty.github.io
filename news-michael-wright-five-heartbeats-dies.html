Michael Wright, ‘The Five Heartbeats’ Star, Dies at 70

Entertainment News

Published: Saturday, August 22, 2026

Michael Wright, the acclaimed actor whose powerful performance as Eddie King Jr. in The Five Heartbeats made him a lasting favorite among movie audiences, has died at 70.

Wright died Wednesday in Los Angeles, according to his family. A statement provided to TMZ said he suffered heart failure and complications related to Marchiafava-Bignami disease, a rare neurological disorder.

His death marks the loss of a versatile performer whose career stretched across nearly five decades and included memorable roles in film, television and theater.

A Career Built on Range

Born April 30, 1956, in New York City, Wright entered professional acting during the late 1970s. One of his earliest notable screen appearances came in The Wanderers, the 1979 coming-of-age drama.

His career quickly expanded, and Wright became known for taking on demanding characters with intensity and authority. His work in Robert Altman’s Streamers in 1983 brought him international recognition and earned him the Volpi Cup for Best Actor at the Venice Film Festival.

Throughout the following decades, Wright continued moving between movies and television. He appeared in The Principal, where he played the imposing Victor Duncan, and later shared the screen with Wesley Snipes in Sugar Hill.

His other credits included films such as Money Talks, Point Blank and The Interpreter. On television, he appeared in productions including HBO’s prison drama Oz and the science-fiction series V: The Series.

Eddie King Jr. Becomes a Career-Defining Performance

Despite his extensive résumé, Wright’s name became especially connected to one character: Eddie King Jr. in Robert Townsend’s 1991 musical drama The Five Heartbeats.

The movie centers on a fictional R&B group navigating fame, friendship, competition and the difficult realities of the music business. Wright played Eddie, the group’s gifted lead vocalist whose immense talent existed alongside addiction, personal turmoil and increasingly destructive choices.

His portrayal gave the character emotional weight beyond the typical rise-and-fall story. Eddie was ambitious and charismatic, but he was also vulnerable, flawed and deeply affected by the pressures surrounding him.

Although The Five Heartbeats was not an enormous box-office success when it was first released, its reputation grew steadily over the years. The movie eventually became a beloved cult classic, particularly among fans of classic R&B, soul music and Black cinema.

For many viewers, Wright’s performance became one of the film’s defining elements.

More Than The Five Heartbeats

Wright’s career should not be reduced to Eddie King Jr.

Over the course of his career, he repeatedly demonstrated an ability to disappear into dramatically different characters. He played criminals, authority figures, troubled men and antagonists while maintaining a distinctive screen presence.

His work in Streamers remains one of the strongest examples of his dramatic ability, while performances in The Principal and Sugar Hill showcased another side of his range.

His television appearances further demonstrated his versatility. From the gritty world of Oz to the science-fiction setting of V: The Series, Wright was able to move comfortably between genres.

That consistency helped him maintain a career across several generations of Hollywood.

A Lasting Connection With Audiences

Years after The Five Heartbeats reached theaters, Wright continued to discuss his career and experiences in the entertainment industry. A 2021 profile highlighted the actor as an underappreciated talent whose body of work extended well beyond his best-known role.

For longtime fans, however, Eddie King Jr. remained particularly special.

The character’s struggles gave Wright the opportunity to portray the complicated relationship between talent, fame and personal demons. His performance helped make the fictional group feel authentic and gave the movie an emotional center that continues to resonate with audiences.

The film’s continued popularity has introduced Wright’s work to viewers who were not even born when the movie originally premiered.

Family Remembers the Actor

Wright’s wife, Susan Wright, paid tribute to him following his death, remembering the man behind the performances as well as the actor audiences knew from the screen.

His family said Wright is survived by his wife, daughter and stepchildren.

Additional details regarding memorial arrangements and plans to celebrate his life are expected to be shared by his family.

A Legacy That Extends Across Generations

News of Wright’s passing prompted renewed appreciation for a career that encompassed much more than a single film.

For some fans, he will always be Eddie King Jr., the magnetic singer at the heart of The Five Heartbeats. For others, his performances in Streamers, The Principal, Sugar Hill, Oz and his many other projects serve as reminders of how much ground he covered as an actor.

Wright’s ability to bring intensity and humanity to difficult characters made him a distinctive presence on screen. His performances will continue to live through the movies and television programs that introduced him to audiences over the years.

Michael Wright leaves behind a body of work spanning generations. While Eddie King Jr. may remain his most recognizable character, his nearly 50-year career reflects an actor whose talents reached far beyond one unforgettable performance.

He was 70.
