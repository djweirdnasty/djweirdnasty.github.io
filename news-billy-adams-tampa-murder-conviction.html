Tampa Rapper Convicted of Killing Pregnant Woman and Her Unborn Child, Faces Death Penalty Phase

National | Crime

Published: Tuesday, September 1, 2026

A Tampa jury has found rapper Billy Adams III guilty of murdering 22-year-old Alana Sims and her unborn child in a case that prosecutors said stemmed from a pregnancy Adams believed involved him.

Jurors reached their verdict Tuesday after several hours of deliberation, convicting Adams of first-degree murder in Sims’ death as well as the death of her unborn child.

Adams, 28, who performed under the name Ace NH, showed little reaction as the verdict was announced in court. The decision now moves the case into a separate sentencing phase, during which prosecutors are expected to seek the death penalty.

Sims was found dead on Jan. 30, 2023, near her SUV in the Easton Park subdivision in New Tampa. She had been shot in the head and was approximately five months pregnant, according to authorities and testimony presented during the trial.

Her 18-month-old child was inside the vehicle at the time and was not physically injured.

Prosecutors’ theory of the case

Prosecutors argued that Adams intentionally brought Sims to the unfinished residential area before killing her. They told jurors that Adams believed he was the father of her unborn child and wanted to avoid responsibility for the pregnancy.

The state also presented evidence that Adams and another woman, Lakenya Brantley, were in a relationship. Prosecutors pointed to messages exchanged shortly before Sims’ death as evidence that the pregnancy had become a source of tension.

The defense disputed the prosecution’s explanation for the killing and maintained that the evidence did not establish a pregnancy-related motive.

Adams’ attorneys also challenged the reliability of witnesses and questioned the lack of physical evidence directly connecting him to the shooting scene.

Conflicting accounts of the shooting

Investigators questioned Adams after Sims was killed. His account of what happened changed during multiple interviews.

At one point, Adams told detectives that Sims had confronted him with a firearm and that the shooting occurred during a struggle. Police later had him demonstrate his version of the encounter using a training weapon.

According to trial testimony, Adams eventually told investigators that he had taken the gun from Sims before shooting her.

Authorities searched an area where Adams reportedly said he had disposed of the firearm, but the weapon was never recovered.

Prosecutors also presented evidence involving Adams’ internet searches around the time of the killing, including searches concerning gunshot residue and Florida arrest procedures.

Jailhouse witness takes the stand

A major part of the prosecution’s case came from Bryan Boggs, a man who was incarcerated with Adams following the killing.

Boggs testified that Adams discussed the case with him and described a potential self-defense explanation. He also claimed Adams had written letters intended to influence what several people would tell investigators about his whereabouts.

Boggs acknowledged during testimony that he had a substantial criminal record and that he hoped providing information to investigators could benefit him. The defense used those circumstances to attack his credibility.

Prosecutors, however, argued that Boggs provided details that supported other evidence collected during the investigation.

Previous murder acquittal

The conviction came only days after a significant earlier development in Adams’ legal history.

In January 2023, Adams was acquitted of murdering two men who were killed at a Lutz recording studio in 2020. Adams had maintained that he shot the men in self-defense after believing they intended to rob the studio owner.

Because of that earlier acquittal, jurors in the Sims case were not told about the previous murder prosecution.

Sims was killed just three days after Adams was acquitted in that case.

Sentencing phase begins Wednesday

With the guilty verdict now entered, the trial moves into its penalty phase.

Prosecutors are expected to present arguments for a death sentence beginning Wednesday morning. The same jury will consider the evidence before making its recommendation regarding punishment.

The final sentencing decision will come later through the court.

The conviction and allegations described in the case are based on evidence and testimony presented during the trial.
