Former Chick-fil-A Worker Sentenced After $80,000 Mac and Cheese Refund Scheme

National | Crime

Published: Friday, August 28, 2026

A former Chick-fil-A employee in Texas has been sentenced to prison after investigators said he exploited the restaurant’s point-of-sale system to generate hundreds of fraudulent refunds totaling more than $80,000.

Keyshun Jones, 23, admitted to carrying out the scheme at a Chick-fil-A restaurant in Grapevine, Texas, after his employment with the company ended.

According to authorities, the scheme involved creating roughly 800 transactions for catering trays of macaroni and cheese and directing the resulting refunds to Jones’ personal credit cards.

Jones was arrested in April following a months-long investigation. He later pleaded guilty and received a three-year prison sentence, with credit for time already served.

How investigators say the scheme worked

The investigation began after the restaurant’s ownership reported suspected theft in November 2025.

Investigators reviewed surveillance footage from the restaurant and allegedly identified Jones entering an area of the business where he was no longer authorized to work.

Police said the footage showed him operating a register and repeatedly entering orders for large quantities of Chick-fil-A macaroni and cheese.

The transactions were subsequently refunded to cards connected to Jones, according to investigators.

Authorities determined that the fraudulent activity resulted in losses exceeding $80,000.

The unusual choice of menu item was later explained by Jones, who told the New York Post that macaroni and cheese was among the restaurant’s more expensive catering offerings, making individual transactions capable of producing relatively large refunds.

Former employee says anger played a role

Jones said he had worked for Chick-fil-A for years and described the restaurant as an important part of his early working life.

In an interview from jail, he disputed the idea that he entered the restaurant with a detailed plan to steal tens of thousands of dollars.

Jones said he had returned to the restaurant with friends after losing his job and saw an opportunity when he encountered an unattended register.

He acknowledged that his actions were wrong and attributed his decision in part to anger following the loss of his job.

The surveillance footage became a key piece of evidence for investigators as they reconstructed the transactions.

Arrest came months after investigation began

Authorities said Jones did not immediately surrender after investigators began attempting to locate him.

Police ultimately arrested him April 17, 2026, with assistance from the Texas Attorney General’s Fugitive Unit and the Fort Worth Police Department.

The initial investigation resulted in allegations including theft of property, money laundering and evading arrest.

The case was later resolved through a plea agreement.

Prosecutors had sought a substantially longer sentence, but Jones ultimately received three years in prison under the agreement reached with his defense attorney and prosecutors.

Jones says he wants another chance

Despite the criminal case and the end of his employment, Jones said he still has affection for the restaurant chain where he began working as a teenager.

He told the Post that he hopes to eventually rebuild his life and potentially return to the food-service industry after completing his sentence.

Jones also said he hopes people will view the offense as a serious mistake rather than a complete reflection of his character.

His wife, Keana Jones, similarly defended him during the interview, describing him as a kind person.

Whether Jones will ever return to his former employer remains uncertain. His previous employer declined to comment on the case.

For now, the former employee will serve his sentence after a scheme that investigators say turned hundreds of fake catering transactions into more than $80,000 in fraudulent refunds.
