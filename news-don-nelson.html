The NBA Loses a Basketball Visionary: Don Nelson Dies at 86

Sports | NBA

Published: Sunday, August 9, 2026

The basketball world is mourning the loss of Don Nelson, one of the NBA's most accomplished coaches and most influential innovators. Nelson died Sunday at the age of 86, surrounded by members of his family.

Nelson's impact on professional basketball can be measured in championships, victories and awards, but his greatest contribution may have been the way he challenged the traditional ideas of how the game should be played.

Over more than three decades on NBA sidelines, Nelson coached the Milwaukee Bucks, Golden State Warriors, New York Knicks and Dallas Mavericks. He finished his coaching career with 1,335 regular-season victories, a total that stood as the NBA's all-time record until Gregg Popovich moved past him in 2022.

Nelson was named NBA Coach of the Year three times and made the playoffs 18 times as a head coach. Although he never captured an NBA championship as a coach, his influence on the league remained significant long after his coaching career ended.

His basketball philosophy became famously associated with the term "Nellieball." Nelson wasn't afraid to use smaller lineups, athletic players and unconventional strategies to create matchup problems for opposing teams. Ideas that once seemed unusual became increasingly common as the NBA evolved into the fast-paced, perimeter-oriented league fans see today.

Nelson's basketball success began long before he became a coach. As a member of the Boston Celtics, he won five NBA championships during an 11-season playing career. Boston later honored him by retiring his No. 19 jersey.

His influence was also felt in Dallas. As coach of the Mavericks, Nelson helped change the direction of the franchise, including playing a key role in the acquisition of future Hall of Famers Dirk Nowitzki and Steve Nash.

Nelson's résumé also includes two NBA All-Star coaching appearances and a gold medal with the United States at the 1994 FIBA World Championship.

The Golden State Warriors, one of the franchises most closely associated with Nelson, described him as a basketball visionary whose willingness to challenge conventional thinking helped influence future generations of the sport.

Away from basketball, Nelson eventually settled on a farm in Maui, Hawaii. He became involved in agriculture and entrepreneurship, growing flowers and coffee and later discussing his cannabis operation. Following the devastating Maui wildfires in 2023, Nelson also opened rental properties to families who had lost their homes.

Don Nelson leaves behind a legacy that goes beyond the numbers. He was a champion as a player, a record-setting coach and an innovator whose ideas helped push professional basketball toward its modern era.

The NBA has lost one of its great thinkers, but Don Nelson's influence on the game will continue to be felt for generations.
