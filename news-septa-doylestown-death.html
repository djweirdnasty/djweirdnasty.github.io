25-Year-Old Man Dies After Climbing Onto SEPTA Train in Doylestown

National | Transit & Public Safety

Published: Wednesday, August 13, 2026

A 25-year-old man died Tuesday evening after climbing onto the roof of a SEPTA Regional Rail train at the Doylestown station and coming into contact with an energized overhead wire, authorities said.

The incident happened shortly after 6 p.m. Aug. 11 at the SEPTA station on South Clinton Street in Doylestown Borough.

How the Incident Unfolded

According to Central Bucks Regional Police, the man had attempted to board a train but was unable to enter because the doors were closed. Police said he then climbed onto the train, where he came into contact with an electrical wire.

The electrical shock caused the man to suffer cardiac arrest, authorities said. He was found on the side of the train opposite the passenger platform as emergency crews arrived.

Emergency Response

Central Bucks EMS and firefighters provided emergency treatment and transported him to Penn Medicine Doylestown Health. Despite efforts to save him, the man was pronounced dead at the hospital.

Authorities have not publicly identified the victim. Police said he was not a Doylestown resident and that his identity would be withheld pending notification of his family and an autopsy.

Several emergency agencies responded, including Central Bucks Regional Police, Central Bucks EMS, the Doylestown Fire Company and SEPTA Transit Police.

SEPTA temporarily shut down electrical power in the area to allow first responders to safely reach the victim and provide medical assistance. Officials said the emergency response was cleared shortly after 6:30 p.m.

Investigation

Police said investigators are not currently looking for additional witnesses or video related to the incident.

The investigation remains ongoing as authorities work to determine the circumstances surrounding the man’s actions before the fatal electrocution.
