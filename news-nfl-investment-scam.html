Man at Center of Alleged NFL Investment Scam Dies as Players Seek Millions in Lost Money

Sports News

Published: Tuesday, August 12, 2026

Former professional football players and other investors are trying to determine whether they can recover hundreds of thousands of dollars after the New Jersey man accused of handling their investments was found dead.

Mohamed Coulibaly, a New Jersey resident in his 20s, was discovered in a swimming pool at a Harrison Township residence on July 31. Police were reportedly sent to the property after members of his family requested a welfare check.

Officials have not released a public explanation for Coulibaly's death. The Gloucester County Prosecutor's Office has also said it has no additional information to release at this time.

The investigation into the financial allegations began before Coulibaly died.

According to a complaint filed with federal and state authorities, Coulibaly operated a business known as Motion Venture. The company reportedly marketed investment opportunities involving online businesses that operated through Shopify.

Former NFL linebacker Tae Crowder is among the investors who say they lost significant amounts of money. Crowder told ESPN that he put about $500,000 into the venture.

Former NFL running back Matt Breida reportedly invested $250,000. Another former NFL player, who was not publicly identified, reportedly invested approximately $675,000. Based on the amounts described in the complaint, those three players alone had more than $1.4 million tied up in the venture.

The investment model reportedly involved people putting money toward online stores. Some investors said they initially saw returns, which helped convince them to put additional funds into the business.

Motion Venture also allegedly offered a larger pooled investment option. Investors were reportedly told they could eventually receive their original contributions back, while the company sought to grow its operations to a much higher valuation.

For some investors, however, the expected payments never came.

Several investors said they began asking Coulibaly for their money once their agreements reached the point when payments were supposed to be made. They said they were repeatedly told that financial institutions, overseas transactions or other banking issues were delaying the process.

Crowder said he eventually involved an attorney and sent a formal demand for the money in May.

The former Giants player said he had reservations about making the investment but was influenced by the people connected to the company. Promotional material reportedly displayed the names of professional athletes and other recognizable figures.

Among the names appearing in company materials were Seattle Seahawks executive John Schneider, former NBA All-Star John Wall and entertainers YG and Tee Grizzley, along with multiple professional football players.

The presence of those names does not necessarily mean they endorsed the business or were investors. It remains unclear whether some individuals listed in the company's materials even knew their names were being used.

A Seahawks representative confirmed that Schneider did invest and lost money. He was reportedly introduced to the opportunity by Steve Keim, a former Arizona Cardinals general manager.

Keim had an official role with Motion Venture and said he promoted the company because he believed it was a legitimate investment. He now says he also suffered a loss totaling more than $1 million.

Keim said he eventually became suspicious about the company's finances. He traveled to Philadelphia shortly before Coulibaly's death in an attempt to speak with banking officials and get a clearer picture of what was happening.

According to Keim, he later informed investors about his concerns and told them he hoped they would be able to recover their money.

Questions were also raised about the online stores themselves. Investigators cited in the complaint reportedly found no clear evidence that the stores were producing enough legitimate revenue to justify the returns investors had been promised.

Previous reporting by Barron's also raised concerns about sales data displayed through the stores, including allegations that certain transactions may have been entered manually rather than generated through actual customer purchases.

Coulibaly rejected the accusations before his death. He told the New York Post that he disagreed with the claims being made about his company. He also said the delayed investor payments were connected to expected money from a potential acquisition.

At the time of his death, no criminal charges had been publicly filed against Coulibaly in connection with the allegations.

His death could make efforts to recover the disputed investments more complicated. Crowder's attorney said investors may still have the option of pursuing claims against Coulibaly's estate, though it is not yet known whether sufficient assets exist to repay those who say they lost money.

Crowder said he had spoken with federal investigators before Coulibaly's death.

Now, investors are left waiting for answers about what happened to their money, what happened inside Motion Venture and whether any funds can ultimately be recovered.

The allegations remain allegations, and no criminal conviction has been entered against Coulibaly.
