Terrion Arnold Reportedly Set to Join Seahawks While Facing Serious Felony Charges

Sports | NFL

Published: Wednesday, August 13, 2026

Former Detroit Lions cornerback Terrion Arnold is reportedly preparing to continue his NFL career with the Seattle Seahawks despite facing eight felony charges connected to an alleged kidnapping and armed robbery scheme.

Multiple reports indicate that Arnold and the Seahawks have reached an agreement on a deal. The reported move comes less than two months after the Lions released the former first-round pick following his arrest in Florida.

Arrest and Charges

Arnold, 23, was taken into custody on June 24 after surrendering to authorities. Prosecutors charged him with four counts of kidnapping and four counts of armed robbery. He was initially held without bond before being released five days later after posting $1 million.

Because Arnold remains out on bond, he is currently permitted to travel for employment-related purposes while his criminal case moves forward. He has reportedly met with several NFL organizations during that time, including Seattle, New Orleans, Houston and the New York Giants.

Arnold’s next court appearance was scheduled for Thursday, with the hearing taking place remotely. Another hearing is scheduled for October 5 in Tampa and is expected to require Arnold’s in-person attendance.

The Alleged Incident

The charges stem from an incident in Largo, Florida, in February. Authorities allege that Arnold and several associates attempted to track down three men they believed had stolen more than $250,000 worth of cash and personal belongings from Arnold and others staying at an Airbnb.

According to prosecutors, the situation escalated when the three men were allegedly taken to an apartment, assaulted and held at gunpoint. Investigators say Arnold was not physically inside the apartment during the alleged attack but monitored the incident through FaceTime.

Authorities also reportedly recovered a group chat in which Arnold and childhood friend Boakai Hilton Jr. allegedly communicated with other people involved in the incident and provided instructions.

Police have described Arnold as an alleged central figure in the investigation. Arnold has denied involvement and maintains that he is innocent.

Co-Defendants

Several other people were arrested in connection with the case, including Hilton, Lyndell Hudson, Freddie Lee Hughes III, Christion Williams, Arianna Del Valle and Jasmine Randazzo.

Del Valle and Randazzo have reportedly entered guilty pleas and agreed to cooperate with prosecutors. Randazzo received a four-year prison sentence followed by six years of probation, while Del Valle’s sentencing remains pending.

Seahawks Signing

Arnold’s legal team has strongly disputed the allegations, arguing that prosecutors are relying on testimony from people who were themselves charged in the case and could have reasons to shift responsibility.

Despite the criminal case, Arnold remains eligible to pursue employment with an NFL team while released on bond. His agent, Nicole Lynn, previously testified that multiple franchises had expressed interest in her client.

However, signing with Seattle does not necessarily mean Arnold will be available for the entire season. The NFL could review the case under its personal conduct policy, which gives the league authority to discipline players accused of conduct considered detrimental to the league.

Arnold could also potentially be placed on the Commissioner’s Exempt List depending on how the league handles the allegations.

Football Background

Before his legal troubles, Arnold was considered an important young defensive prospect. Detroit selected him with the 24th overall pick in the 2024 NFL Draft, and he appeared in 24 games during his two seasons with the Lions.

His 2025 campaign ended prematurely after he underwent shoulder surgery in December. Before the injury, he recorded 23 solo tackles and one interception through eight games.

What’s Next

The Seahawks’ reported interest represents a significant opportunity for Arnold to return to the field, but his future remains complicated by the pending criminal case.

Arnold’s attorneys have maintained that he had no involvement in the alleged crimes and have said he expects the court process to ultimately clear his name. The charges remain allegations, and Arnold has not been convicted of the offenses.
