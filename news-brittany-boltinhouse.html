Crown Taken Away, Legal Fight May Be Next for Former Miss North Carolina USA

Entertainment | Pageants

Published: Wednesday, August 13, 2026

Brittany Boltinhouse is preparing to challenge the circumstances surrounding the loss of her Miss North Carolina USA title, with her lawyer alleging that her religious and political beliefs played a role in the decision.

Attorney Patrick Mincey announced that he is representing Boltinhouse and said his firm is examining possible legal claims related to the pageant’s decision. He characterized the removal as unfair and said Boltinhouse was singled out because she is a Christian with conservative views.

Boltinhouse was removed from the title on Aug. 5, only weeks before she was expected to participate in the Miss USA competition later this month. Myla Hadley, who finished as runner-up, has assumed the North Carolina title.

The Miss USA organization has maintained that the decision was based on information that surfaced after Boltinhouse won the crown. Officials said they conducted a review before determining that her alleged conduct did not meet the organization’s standards.

Pageant officials referenced policies concerning racism, homophobia and transphobia but did not publicly identify the specific behavior or statements that prompted the decision.

Thom Brodeur, the organization’s chairman and CEO, said the issue involved more than an isolated incident. According to Brodeur, the concerns involved behavior that allegedly occurred over a longer period and conflicted with the expectations attached to representing Miss USA.

A Blaize Productions, which handles Miss USA preliminary competitions in North Carolina and other states, similarly defended the move. The company said officials considered the circumstances as a whole and believed removing Boltinhouse was necessary to protect the credibility of the state title.

Boltinhouse disputes that explanation.

Speaking with the Carolina Journal, she said she believes her conservative Christian beliefs were behind the decision to remove her. She also said an NDA prevents her from discussing every detail surrounding the controversy.

Boltinhouse was asked whether her support for President Donald Trump or her conservative political positions could have contributed to the decision. She said she did not know for certain, while maintaining that she believes her beliefs ultimately cost her the crown.

Mincey said his firm is now looking into how the decision was reached and whether Boltinhouse’s rights were violated. He has not confirmed whether the eventual legal action will specifically target Miss USA, A Blaize Productions or another party.

The attorney also pushed back against the idea that Boltinhouse should simply move on from the controversy. He argued that she earned the title and the support that came with it and said she intends to defend her reputation.

For now, the exact nature of any lawsuit remains uncertain. No formal legal complaint has been publicly identified, and the organizations involved have not announced any settlement or agreement with Boltinhouse.

The controversy comes just weeks before the national Miss USA competition, transforming what was supposed to be Boltinhouse’s opportunity to compete on the national stage into a dispute over pageant rules, alleged misconduct, religious beliefs and free expression.

Miss USA and Miss America are separate pageant organizations and are not affiliated with one another.
