Navy Considers Renaming Carrier Named for Pearl Harbor Hero Doris Miller

National News

Published: Friday, August 21, 2026

A controversy is developing around a future U.S. Navy aircraft carrier that was originally named after Doris “Dorie” Miller, a Black sailor whose actions during the attack on Pearl Harbor made him one of the most recognized enlisted heroes of World War II.

The Ford-class carrier, currently identified by its hull number CVN-81, was announced in 2020 as the future USS Doris Miller. According to reports, however, Navy officials are now considering changing the ship’s name before construction reaches its keel-laying ceremony.

Sources familiar with internal discussions told CNN that President Donald Trump has been discussed as a possible namesake for the carrier. If that happens, it would represent an unusual departure from the Navy’s traditional practice, as no U.S. aircraft carrier has previously been named for a sitting president.

Why Doris Miller’s Name Matters

Miller was born in Waco, Texas, and joined the Navy in 1939. At the time, Black sailors faced significant restrictions on the jobs they could hold, and Miller served as a mess attendant.

When Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, Miller was performing laundry duties when the attack began. After reaching the deck, he helped move his wounded commanding officer to safety.

Miller then took control of an anti-aircraft machine gun despite having no formal training with the weapon. Navy records say he continued firing at Japanese aircraft until he ran out of ammunition and the ship began sinking.

He also helped fellow sailors escape the damaged vessel.

Miller was later awarded the Navy Cross for his actions. He was killed in 1943 during the Battle of Makin after his ship, the USS Liscome Bay, was struck by a Japanese torpedo.

His story was later portrayed by actor Cuba Gooding Jr. in the 2001 film Pearl Harbor.

Family Was Not Told About Potential Name Change

Miller’s descendants have reportedly spent years advocating for him to receive the Medal of Honor, the nation’s highest military decoration for valor.

Thomas Bledsoe, Miller’s great-nephew, said the family had not been notified about the reported effort to change the carrier’s name or the renewed recommendation for the Medal of Honor.

Bledsoe described the potential Medal of Honor recommendation as a positive development while expressing surprise over the possible renaming of the carrier.

The Navy is reportedly considering naming another warship after Miller if the carrier’s name is changed.

Trump Name Would Be Unprecedented for a Carrier

Aircraft carriers have historically carried the names of former presidents, senior military leaders and other major figures.

Examples include the USS Abraham Lincoln, USS George Washington, USS Ronald Reagan and USS George H.W. Bush. Other carriers have been named after military figures, including Admiral Chester Nimitz.

The USS Doris Miller would have broken another tradition by becoming the first U.S. aircraft carrier named for an enlisted sailor and the first carrier named for an African American.

Naming the ship after Trump while he is still president would create another historic first.

Navy Reviewing Ship-Naming Policy

The reported reconsideration comes after the Navy established a commission to examine the way military ships and other assets are named.

Former Acting Secretary of the Navy John Phelan reportedly favored limiting aircraft-carrier names to presidents, Navy admirals and major Navy battles. A study of the issue was commissioned, although Phelan left his position before the review was completed.

The Navy has also undergone broader changes involving the names of military installations and vessels during the Trump administration.

The future carrier is currently still under construction and is not expected to enter service until the 2030s. Its keel-laying ceremony is expected later this year, putting pressure on Navy officials to settle the name before that public milestone.

Debate Over Miller’s Legacy

The possibility of changing the carrier’s name has already generated criticism from some lawmakers and veterans’ advocates.

Former astronaut and retired Navy officer Senator Mark Kelly said Miller’s bravery should continue to be recognized through the carrier that was originally named for him.

Meanwhile, Navy officials have not publicly confirmed that Trump will become the ship’s namesake. The Department of Defense has said there is currently nothing to announce.

For now, CVN-81 remains the ship’s official hull designation while questions surrounding its eventual name continue.

Any final decision would determine whether the Navy’s next Ford-class carrier carries forward the name of a decorated Black enlisted sailor who became a symbol of courage at Pearl Harbor—or receives a different name altogether.
