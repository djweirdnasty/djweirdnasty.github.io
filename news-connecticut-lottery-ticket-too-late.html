Connecticut Man Missed $5.8 Million Lottery Jackpot After Discovering Winning Ticket Too Late

National | Lottery

Published: Sunday, August 23, 2026

For Clarence Jackson, a routine lottery purchase turned into one of the most painful near-misses imaginable.

Jackson, a Connecticut man who was working as a cleaner while caring for his seriously ill father, purchased a Quick Pick lottery ticket on October 13, 1995. One year later, he discovered that the numbers printed on that ticket had matched all six numbers needed to win a jackpot worth approximately $5.8 million.

There was just one problem: Jackson discovered the winning ticket on the very last day he was legally allowed to claim the prize.

And circumstances surrounding that deadline ultimately meant the fortune would remain unclaimed.

A winning ticket hidden in plain sight

Jackson bought the Quick Pick ticket in October 1995, apparently without realizing that it would eventually become one of the most valuable pieces of paper he had ever possessed.

At the time, however, his attention was largely focused elsewhere. His father was battling emphysema and spent much of the following year hospitalized. Jackson later explained that he had devoted significant time to caring for him.

By the time Jackson realized he had the winning numbers, nearly the entire one-year claim period had passed.

October 13, 1996, marked the final day of Connecticut’s 365-day deadline for claiming the prize. But there was another complication: The date fell on a Sunday.

Jackson believed he needed to take the ticket directly to the Connecticut lottery headquarters. Assuming the office would be closed for the weekend, he did not submit the ticket that day.

What Jackson did not know was that winning tickets could be presented at the retailer where they had originally been purchased.

A holiday created another obstacle

The following day did little to improve his situation.

October 14 was Columbus Day, a state holiday in Connecticut. As a result, Jackson did not present his ticket until October 15 — two days after the one-year deadline had expired.

By then, the Connecticut Lottery Corporation had no choice under its rules but to reject his claim.

The missed deadline transformed what should have been a multimillion-dollar payday into a legal dispute.

Jackson subsequently filed a lawsuit against the Connecticut Lottery Corporation, arguing that the circumstances surrounding his father’s illness and his misunderstanding of the claim process should be taken into consideration.

His legal team maintained that his situation was extraordinary and that he should not lose millions because of circumstances surrounding the deadline.

The lottery, however, relied on the rules governing the ticket.

The court sided with the lottery

The legal argument ultimately failed.

The Connecticut Lottery Corporation maintained that the ticket’s terms gave winners a full calendar year to claim their prizes. Because Jackson did not submit the ticket within that period, the lottery argued that he was no longer entitled to the jackpot.

The court agreed with the lottery.

Jackson therefore lost his claim to the approximately $5.8 million prize — a fortune that, had it been collected, could have dramatically altered the course of his life.

His story became one of the more striking examples of how a lottery winner can technically possess a winning ticket yet still walk away with nothing.

Jackson later found a different perspective

Despite the enormous financial loss, Jackson eventually developed a surprisingly philosophical view of what happened.

During a 2016 appearance connected to Good Morning America, he reflected on what might have happened if he had actually received the money as a younger man.

Rather than dwelling solely on the fortune he missed, Jackson suggested that receiving millions at that stage of his life might have created problems of its own.

He explained that he had been pursuing a career in music at the time and believed he was not mature enough to handle such a large sum responsibly.

Jackson said he might have ended up “dead or broke” if he had received the jackpot.

His comments offered an unusual conclusion to a story that initially appeared to be nothing but tragedy.

A lottery lesson about deadlines

Jackson’s experience also illustrates a critical part of playing the lottery that can be overshadowed by the excitement of winning: a ticket is only valuable if it is claimed within the applicable deadline.

Lottery rules vary by state, including how long winners have to collect prizes and where tickets can be redeemed. In Jackson’s case, his misunderstanding about where to claim the ticket became especially consequential because his discovery occurred on the final day of the claim period.

His father’s illness also played a major role in the circumstances surrounding the missed deadline. Jackson said his father had been hospitalized for most of the year and had only returned home about a week before the ticket expired.

Ultimately, the jackpot remained out of Jackson’s reach.

What began with a Quick Pick purchase in 1995 became a story about illness, timing, legal deadlines and an extraordinary stroke of bad luck — followed years later by Jackson’s own belief that losing millions might have saved him from making mistakes with money when he was young.

For most lottery players, matching six winning numbers is the dream. Jackson accomplished that part.

He simply discovered it too late.
