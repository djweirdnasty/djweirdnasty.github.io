Luigi Mangione Could Change His Plea as Federal Case Takes New Turn

National | Crime

Published: Wednesday, August 13, 2026

Luigi Mangione could enter a guilty plea in Manhattan federal court Friday in the case stemming from the fatal shooting of UnitedHealthcare executive Brian Thompson, according to people familiar with the matter.

Mangione, 28, is expected to appear before a federal judge as negotiations between prosecutors and his defense team continue. However, the agreement is not considered final, and the details of any potential plea have not been publicly released.

The development comes shortly before Mangione is scheduled to face a separate prosecution in New York state court. His state trial is currently scheduled to begin Sept. 8.

Thompson was killed on Dec. 4, 2024, outside a Manhattan hotel where UnitedHealthcare was holding an investor conference. The shooting launched a nationwide search for the gunman and quickly became one of the most closely watched criminal cases in the country.

Mangione was arrested days later at a McDonald’s in Altoona, Pennsylvania. Authorities said investigators recovered items they believe connected him to the shooting, including a notebook prosecutors have described as containing writings related to the alleged plan.

Federal Charges and Double Jeopardy Questions

The federal case has already undergone significant changes. Prosecutors originally charged Mangione with federal murder and weapons offenses, exposing him to the possibility of a death sentence. In January 2026, U.S. District Judge Margaret Garnett dismissed those charges.

The remaining federal case centers on two stalking allegations tied to Thompson’s death. Each charge carries the possibility of a life sentence.

It is not yet known whether Mangione plans to admit guilt to both charges or reach an agreement involving only one. His decision could also change before Friday’s hearing, as plea negotiations can continue until a defendant formally enters a plea in court.

State Case Still Ahead

The potential federal resolution could have an impact beyond the federal courthouse.

Mangione’s attorneys have argued that allowing both the federal government and New York prosecutors to pursue cases arising from the same shooting violates double-jeopardy protections. His defense is expected to renew that argument if he accepts a federal plea agreement.

A federal plea, however, would not automatically end the state prosecution. Prosecutors could argue that the federal stalking allegations and New York’s murder case represent separate legal offenses, leaving the courts to determine whether the state proceedings can continue.

Mangione’s New York case includes a second-degree murder charge as well as weapons-related counts. He has pleaded not guilty, and a conviction on the murder charge could result in a sentence of 25 years to life.

The Manhattan District Attorney’s Office has not commented on the possibility of a federal plea.

National Reaction

The shooting and subsequent arrest generated an unusually intense public reaction. Thompson’s death sparked discussions about the U.S. health insurance industry, while Mangione himself became a controversial public figure, drawing supporters to court appearances and online attention.

If Mangione enters a guilty plea Friday, the federal case could move toward sentencing while his defense continues its fight over whether New York can proceed with its own prosecution.

With the state trial only weeks away, the federal plea decision could become a pivotal moment in a case that has attracted national attention since Thompson’s death.
