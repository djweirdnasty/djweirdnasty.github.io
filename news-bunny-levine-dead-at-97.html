Bunny Levine Dead at 97: Remembering the ‘Gilmore Girls’ and ‘Everybody Loves Raymond’ Actress

Entertainment | Obituary

Published: Thursday, August 20, 2026

Bunny Levine, the veteran character actress whose career included appearances on Gilmore Girls, Everybody Loves Raymond, Shameless and La La Land, has died at 97.

Levine died on August 20, 2026, at her home in Tarzana, California. She reportedly passed away in her sleep. Police were called to the residence that morning after receiving a report of an unresponsive person, with authorities indicating that the circumstances appeared consistent with natural causes.

While Levine was never typically the leading star of the productions in which she appeared, she became a recognizable and dependable presence across television and film. Her career was also notable because she reached Hollywood after spending much of her adult life in another profession.

A Hollywood Career That Came Later in Life

Born December 22, 1928, in East Orange, New Jersey, Levine developed an interest in performing when she was young. She participated in school and local productions, but acting did not immediately become her profession.

After graduating from Washington State University in 1951, she returned to the East Coast and eventually spent approximately 25 years working as a school librarian and teacher in New Jersey.

During those years, Levine was also raising her family with her husband, Bernard. Although she had stepped away from the entertainment world professionally, the desire to act remained with her.

Following retirement, Levine began taking acting classes and attending auditions. Her first professional screen credit came in the 1988 horror-comedy Slime City. She was 59 at the time.

What might have been a short-lived second career instead became a remarkable decades-long run.

Moving to Los Angeles Changed Everything

After her husband died in 2003, Levine moved to Los Angeles. The relocation put her closer to the center of the entertainment industry and allowed her to pursue acting opportunities with greater intensity.

Commercial work eventually led to television and film appearances, and Levine steadily developed a résumé filled with recognizable productions.

Her career became an example of what could happen when someone refused to view age as a reason to stop pursuing a longtime ambition.

Rather than attempting to become a traditional Hollywood star, Levine found her place as a character actress. She frequently appeared as neighbors, relatives, customers, older women and other supporting personalities who helped make a production’s fictional world feel authentic.

Her ‘Gilmore Girls’ Role

Fans of Gilmore Girls may remember Levine from her appearance as Mrs. Thompson.

The character became involved in one of Luke Danes’ storylines after informing him that circumstances surrounding a property he had been using were changing. Her decision affected Luke’s plans and created another complication for the famously stubborn Stars Hollow diner owner.

It was a supporting appearance, but it was representative of the type of role Levine performed throughout her career.

The world of Gilmore Girls depended heavily on memorable supporting characters, and performers such as Levine helped give the series its distinctive small-town atmosphere.

A Long List of Television Credits

Levine worked across an unusually broad range of television programs.

Her credits included Law & Order, Everybody Loves Raymond, Chappelle’s Show, Las Vegas, Private Practice, Ugly Betty, Community, Criminal Minds, 2 Broke Girls, Raising Hope, Southland, New Girl, The Mindy Project, Fuller House, Shameless, Getting On, Mayans M.C. and other productions.

Her role on Everybody Loves Raymond was another particularly recognizable appearance. Levine played Hilda, a friend of Ray’s mother, Marie.

The role gave her an opportunity to work within the show’s fast-paced brand of family comedy and showcased the sharp timing that would become an important part of her character work.

Her television career also demonstrated her versatility. She could move comfortably between sitcoms, dramas, crime series and contemporary streaming productions.

Working With Adam Sandler

Levine’s film career included several collaborations with Adam Sandler.

She appeared in You Don’t Mess With the Zohan in 2008 and later returned for Sandy Wexler in 2017.

In 2023, she appeared in You Are So Not Invited to My Bat Mitzvah, the Netflix coming-of-age comedy that featured Sandler alongside his daughters and other members of his family.

Her filmography also included The Jimmy Show, A Thousand Words, La La Land, Thelma and Sacramento.

A Small Role in ‘La La Land’

One of Levine’s most recognizable movie credits came in La La Land.

The 2016 musical, starring Ryan Gosling and Emma Stone, became a major critical and commercial success. Levine appeared in a small role as a movie theater cashier.

Although her screen time was limited, the film became one of the defining Hollywood productions of its era and added another high-profile title to Levine’s résumé.

Her participation in the movie was another reminder of how extensive her career had become after starting professionally later than most actors.

She Continued Acting Into Her 90s

Perhaps the most extraordinary aspect of Levine’s career was how long she continued working.

Her résumé did not stop when she reached traditional retirement age. Instead, she continued accepting roles and appearing in new productions well into her 90s.

She appeared in Thelma in 2024 and remained active in television and film afterward. Her later credits included The Upshaws and other projects.

Her final film appearance came in The Cure, released in 2026, in which she played Mrs. Polanski.

That means Levine was still appearing in professionally released projects during the final year of her life.

She Never Lost Sight of Her Original Dream

Levine’s career was unusual not simply because of its length, but because of where it began.

She had already spent decades working in education before stepping in front of the camera professionally. By the time she made her first screen appearance, she was nearly 60.

In a 2025 interview, Levine reflected on the pull she had always felt toward acting. She described the desire to perform as something that had been present for as long as she could remember.

Her philosophy toward the profession was straightforward: continue learning, keep improving and take the opportunities that come along.

That approach allowed her to build a résumé that many younger performers would envy.

A Different Kind of Hollywood Success Story

Levine’s career represents a different version of the Hollywood success story.

She did not arrive in Los Angeles as a young performer looking for her big break. She came after decades of working in education and following a very different path.

Yet she managed to establish herself as a working actress and remained active for more than 35 years.

Her story is particularly meaningful in an industry where age can often become a barrier to opportunity. Levine instead turned the later chapters of her life into a period of professional reinvention.

She demonstrated that an acting career could begin at 59 and continue into someone’s 90s.

Remembering Bunny Levine

For television audiences, Levine will likely be remembered through the many individual characters she brought to life.

Some viewers will recognize her from Gilmore Girls. Others will remember her from Everybody Loves Raymond, Shameless, Ugly Betty, Law & Order, Criminal Minds, New Girl or The Mindy Project.

Movie audiences may know her from La La Land, her Adam Sandler comedies, Thelma or one of her other film appearances.

Taken together, those credits tell the story of a performer who made the most of every opportunity.

Bunny Levine spent the first several decades of her adult life building a career outside Hollywood. Then, when she finally pursued acting professionally, she refused to let her age determine how far she could go.

Her career lasted from her first screen credit in 1988 through projects released in 2026.

At 97, Levine leaves behind a body of work that spans television generations, major motion pictures, independent productions and streaming entertainment.

But beyond the credits, her legacy may be most inspiring for the example she set: sometimes the dream you put aside for years is still waiting for you—and it is never too late to see where it can take you.
