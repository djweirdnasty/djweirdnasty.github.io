Indiana Woman Charged After Fatal Fort Wayne Hit-and-Run Investigation Leads Police to Red Ford F-150

National | Crime

Published: Sunday, August 30, 2026

Investigators say a DoorDash driver was behind the wheel when a pedestrian was fatally struck. Months later, police say vehicle evidence, surveillance footage and witness information helped identify a suspect.

FORT WAYNE, Ind. — A months-long investigation into the death of a Fort Wayne pedestrian has resulted in felony charges against a 26-year-old woman, with investigators alleging that evidence from the crash ultimately led them to a red Ford F-150.

Esperanza M. Villafana has been charged with leaving the scene of an accident resulting in death and obstruction of justice following the June death of 49-year-old Donsia Lamont Kizer, according to court documents and information released by Fort Wayne authorities.

The investigation began after Kizer was discovered unresponsive along a roadway on Fort Wayne’s east side. What initially appeared to be an unexplained fatality eventually developed into a hit-and-run investigation.

Police reconstruct the final minutes

Kizer was found near the intersection of Maysville Road and Lake Forest Drive during the early morning hours of June 19.

Investigators later used data from a fitness application to establish an estimated time of death at approximately 11:54 p.m. on June 18, according to court documents.

Evidence at the scene indicated that Kizer had suffered severe injuries consistent with being struck by a vehicle. Investigators also found debris that they believed came from a Ford F-150 produced between 2009 and 2014.

A shoe belonging to Kizer was located approximately 90 feet from the area where his body was found, adding to investigators’ reconstruction of the collision. Police also reported evidence indicating that Kizer’s body may have been dragged after the impact.

The driver, however, was not immediately identified.

A red truck becomes the focus

Investigators turned to surveillance footage from the surrounding area. Video reportedly showed a red Ford F-150 traveling along Maysville Road shortly before the estimated time of the collision. That became an important lead.

Authorities began examining vehicle registrations and other information to determine who had access to a truck matching the description.

For weeks, the investigation continued. Then investigators received information from a person who told police they had spent time with Villafana before the crash.

According to court documents, the witness told investigators Villafana had consumed alcohol and marijuana before leaving to make DoorDash deliveries. The witness also reportedly provided information connecting Villafana to a red Ford F-150. That tip gave investigators a potential suspect and vehicle to examine more closely.

Villafana’s account of the collision

When investigators eventually questioned Villafana, she reportedly provided her own description of what happened that night.

According to court documents, Villafana said she had been driving south on Maysville Road when she heard a noise and looked downward. She then felt what she believed was an impact.

Villafana reportedly told investigators she did not immediately see what she had struck. Her initial belief, according to police, was that she might have hit a mailbox or an animal.

But investigators say she later returned to the vicinity of the crash with another person. Villafana reportedly told police that she noticed a dark object near the roadway but did not stop because she did not believe it was a person. By then, authorities say Kizer had already been fatally injured.

Police say news reports changed her suspicions

Investigators also questioned Villafana about what she did after the collision.

According to court records, she acknowledged following police activity information after the incident. Police say she was attempting to determine whether the crash involved a person.

Villafana reportedly told investigators that news coverage of the fatal incident contributed to her realizing that she may have struck a pedestrian. Detectives later questioned her about whether the news reports were what caused her to consider that she had hit someone. According to the documents, Villafana indicated that they were.

That alleged sequence became a significant part of the investigation because prosecutors may have to establish what Villafana knew immediately after the collision and what she learned later.

Investigators say the truck was repaired

The condition of the Ford F-150 also became a major component of the case.

Police say the truck had sustained damage during the incident and that several components were subsequently replaced. Investigators identified repairs involving the vehicle’s headlight, grille and hood.

According to court documents, Villafana acknowledged making those repairs. Detectives then questioned her about whether she had done so because she believed law enforcement might be looking for the truck. Police say she agreed that this was a reason for repairing the vehicle.

That allegation forms the basis of the obstruction charge filed against her.

Months of investigation end in arrest

On August 19, police stopped Villafana while she was driving the Ford in Fort Wayne. Authorities had obtained a search warrant for the vehicle, according to court documents.

Investigators also interviewed Villafana after she reportedly agreed to speak with them following a Miranda warning. The investigation continued until authorities took her into custody on August 28.

She now faces two felony counts:

  • Leaving the scene of an accident resulting in death — Level 4 felony
  • Obstruction of justice — Level 6 felony

Villafana was being held in the Allen County Jail, according to authorities. A preliminary court hearing had not yet been scheduled in the latest information provided by police.

The evidence behind the case

The investigation relied on more than a single witness or piece of evidence.

Authorities say they used a combination of surveillance footage, vehicle debris, registration information, digital data, witness statements and statements attributed to Villafana herself. That combination allowed investigators to move from an unidentified vehicle involved in a deadly collision to a specific truck and, eventually, a person prosecutors now accuse of being responsible.

The case also highlights how investigators can continue building a hit-and-run case long after a crash occurs. A vehicle seen on surveillance can become a lead. A piece of damaged equipment can help identify a vehicle model. Digital information can establish a timeline. And statements made weeks later can become evidence investigators use to compare a suspect’s account with the physical evidence.

What happens next

The allegations will now move through Allen County’s court system.

Villafana has been charged, but the accusations against her remain allegations unless and until they are proven in court. Prosecutors will ultimately have to establish the elements of each offense beyond the initial investigative claims.

For Kizer’s family, the case represents the latest development in an investigation that began with a pedestrian found dead beside a Fort Wayne roadway.

For investigators, the focus has shifted from identifying the vehicle involved to proving exactly what happened before, during and after the collision. And for the court, the central issue will be whether the evidence supports the charges filed against Villafana.

The investigation may have taken more than two months to reach an arrest. Now, the next chapter will unfold inside the courtroom.
