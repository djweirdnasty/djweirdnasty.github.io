Five Dead After Amazon Cargo Jet Runs Off Miami Airport Runway

National | Breaking News

Published: Monday, September 7, 2026

MIAMI — A federal investigation is underway after an Amazon-operated cargo plane sped beyond the runway at Miami International Airport Sunday, striking vehicles and leaving five people dead.

The Boeing 767 was completing a flight into Miami when it failed to stop within the runway area shortly before 2 p.m. The aircraft continued across airport grounds, through a fence and toward a nearby roadway used by workers and businesses.

The National Transportation Safety Board is now examining the aircraft's approach, landing and the conditions that existed in the moments before the crash.

NTSB Chairwoman Jennifer Homendy visited the crash site Monday and described the destruction as extensive.

Investigators documented aircraft debris, vehicle parts, damaged fencing and tire marks leading away from the runway.

Cleaning Crew Among Crash Victims

Five people were killed in the accident. According to the NTSB, all five victims were inside a van belonging to Professional Ocean Service Corp., a company that provides cleaning services for airlines.

Seven people were reportedly traveling in the van when it was struck.

Several other people were injured. Officials said three of the injured were in critical condition, while additional victims were taken to hospitals with less serious injuries.

Emergency crews faced a difficult rescue operation, with people trapped in vehicles and parts of the wreckage spread across the area.

NTSB Examines Where Plane Touched Down

One of the central questions in the investigation is how far down the runway the aircraft traveled before its wheels reached the pavement.

Video recorded during the incident appears to show the cargo jet remaining airborne for an extended portion of the runway before touching down.

Investigators will examine the aircraft's flight-data recorder and other evidence to establish its speed, position and performance during the landing.

Weather could also become an important part of the investigation. Although rain was not falling at the time of the accident, storm clouds and strong winds were reported around the airport.

A tailwind, if present, could have increased the aircraft's ground speed and reduced the amount of runway available for stopping.

Investigators will also review the plane's braking system, mechanical condition, maintenance records and the actions taken by the flight crew.

Airport Faces Major Travel Disruptions

The accident temporarily brought operations at Miami International Airport to a standstill and caused widespread problems for travelers.

Two of the airport's four runways remained closed Monday, while cancellations and delays continued to affect flights.

The disruption came during the Labor Day travel period, when airports across the country were handling elevated passenger traffic.

Officials reported hundreds of delayed or canceled flights connected to the crash and its aftermath.

Why Didn't the Runway Have an Arresting System?

Another issue receiving attention is the absence of an engineered aircraft arresting system at the runway involved in the accident.

These systems use specially designed material capable of absorbing an aircraft's energy if it travels beyond the runway. Federal aviation officials say such systems have helped prevent deaths in previous runway-overrun accidents.

Miami International Airport, however, is not required to have one at the affected runway because officials say the airport has the necessary safety area beyond the runway.

The investigation will determine whether that safety area was adequate under the circumstances of Sunday's accident.

Cargo Jet Had Been Converted From Passenger Service

The aircraft was a Boeing 767 that had spent more than two decades in passenger service before being converted into a freighter in 2015.

The roughly 32-year-old aircraft was being operated by 21 Air, a North Carolina-based cargo airline, on behalf of Amazon Air.

Investigators are expected to review the aircraft's maintenance and operating history as they work to establish whether any mechanical or operational issues contributed to the crash.

Amazon said it was cooperating with investigators and offered condolences to the victims and their families.

Investigation Could Take Time

Officials have not identified a definitive cause for the accident.

The NTSB will reconstruct the aircraft's final approach, determine its touchdown point and examine what happened between the initial landing attempt and the moment the aircraft left the runway.

Flight-data recorder information, cockpit evidence, wreckage, surveillance video and witness accounts could all help investigators piece together the final moments of the flight.

The investigation remains active, and officials are expected to release additional information as more evidence is reviewed.
