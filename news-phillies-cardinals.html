Phillies Hold Off Cardinals as Andrew Painter and Alan Rangel Rescue Depleted Bullpen

Sports | MLB

Published: Tuesday, August 11, 2026

The Philadelphia Phillies found a way to protect a narrow lead Monday night despite entering the game with a severely limited bullpen.

Andrew Painter delivered one of his strongest outings since returning from Triple-A, carrying a no-hitter into the sixth inning before Alan Rangel stepped in and provided the Phillies with several critical outs. Philadelphia ultimately held on for a 6-5 victory over the St. Louis Cardinals at Busch Stadium.

Phillies manager Don Mattingly knew before the game that his pitching staff was stretched thin. With several relievers unavailable because of recent workloads, the team needed Painter to provide as much length as possible.

The young right-hander responded by keeping St. Louis without a hit through the first five innings. Painter entered the sixth protecting a 3-0 advantage, but the Cardinals finally broke through after he recorded the inning’s first out.

Painter walked a batter, surrendered a double and then gave up a two-run single that suddenly made the game 3-2. After reaching 93 pitches, Painter’s night was over, and Rangel was called upon to finish the inning.

Rangel did considerably more than that.

The right-hander recorded the final two outs of the sixth and remained on the mound through the eighth inning, ultimately throwing 2 2/3 innings after Painter’s departure. His ability to cover so many outs proved crucial for Philadelphia’s exhausted relief corps.

Painter finished with six strikeouts and three walks. The victory was his second of the season and his first since earning a win in his major-league debut on March 31.

The victory was also significant for the Phillies because it ended a lengthy stretch in which their starting pitchers outside of Zack Wheeler, Cristopher Sanchez, Jesus Luzardo and Aaron Nola had not produced a win. Philadelphia had gone without a victory from another starter since May 18.

The Phillies appeared to have some breathing room late in the game.

Kyle Schwarber’s RBI double in the seventh increased Philadelphia’s advantage, and Edmundo Sosa delivered a two-run double in the eighth to give the Phillies a 6-2 lead.

But the Cardinals refused to go quietly.

Rangel ran into trouble in the bottom of the eighth, allowing five hits and three runs. St. Louis brought the potential tying run into scoring position with runners on second and third, but Rangel managed to escape the inning without surrendering the lead.

The situation was particularly tense because Philadelphia did not have many realistic bullpen options remaining.

Jonathan Bowlan was essentially the team’s final available reliever, and he was eventually called upon to close out the game. The 29-year-old right-hander responded with a quick ninth inning to earn the first save of his major-league career.

The moment was especially meaningful for Bowlan, who grew up near Memphis as a Cardinals supporter. His father, Mark Bowlan, previously pitched in the Cardinals’ minor-league organization, and many of Bowlan’s family members and friends were in attendance.

Bowlan described the night as particularly memorable because of his family being there to watch his first major-league save.

Mattingly said the Phillies were prepared to get creative if the game had gone beyond nine innings. With the bullpen so depleted, catcher Garrett Stubbs could have been an option to pitch in an emergency.

Bowlan made sure that scenario never became necessary.

Painter has now made three starts since returning from Triple-A and has allowed only five runs across 15 1/3 innings. His fastball reached 98.1 mph Monday, while his circle changeup produced three of his strikeouts.

Painter previously relied on a splitter but has moved away from the pitch in favor of the changeup.

For Philadelphia, Monday’s win was about more than simply adding another victory to the standings. The Phillies remained one game ahead of San Diego for the final National League wild-card position with 42 games left in the regular season.

It also provided the pitching staff with something it desperately needed: rest.

Although several relievers received a break Monday, Mattingly acknowledged that the bullpen still isn’t completely recovered. A deep outing from Sanchez in Tuesday night’s game could be especially valuable as Philadelphia continues to manage its pitching workload.

The Phillies may not have had many options Monday night, but Painter, Rangel and Bowlan gave them exactly what they needed to escape St. Louis with an important win.
