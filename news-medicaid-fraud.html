19 Charged in Alleged $4 Million Pennsylvania Medicaid Fraud Scheme

National News

Published: Tuesday, August 11, 2026

Federal and state authorities have announced criminal charges against 19 people and a Philadelphia-area home health care company accused of taking part in a scheme that allegedly generated more than $4 million in fraudulent Medicaid claims.

Investigators say the alleged misconduct involved Pennsylvania’s Medicaid program and included home health aides submitting claims for hours of care that were never actually provided.

Officials announced the charges Tuesday, describing the investigation as part of a larger effort to crack down on fraud involving taxpayer-funded health care programs.

According to authorities, some of the alleged billing involved extreme discrepancies. Investigators said there were cases in which services were reportedly billed by individuals who were deceased, incarcerated or otherwise unable to provide the claimed care. Authorities also alleged that some aides submitted claims for more than 24 hours of work in a single day on repeated occasions.

One investigation reportedly involved two caregivers and two Medicaid recipients. Authorities say the alleged activity involving that group resulted in more than $400,000 in fraudulent claims.

The Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Office said the alleged abuse was extensive and took advantage of a program intended to help elderly and disabled residents receive care in their homes.

A Philadelphia-based home health company, Benevolent Home Health, has also been charged in connection with the investigation. Authorities said the company’s owner was arrested as part of the case.

During the investigation, officials said the owner allegedly responded to the allegations by suggesting that similar practices were widespread. Authorities said that response highlighted the scope of the problem they believe investigators uncovered.

Officials emphasized that the alleged conduct goes beyond the loss of taxpayer money.

Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday said fraudulent billing can have consequences for people who depend on Medicaid services. If money is improperly diverted from the program, officials argue, resources may not reach patients who genuinely need home health assistance.

Federal officials also described the alleged scheme as a serious breach of trust between patients, caregivers and the health care system.

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services said Medicaid programs are intended to provide legitimate support to people who require assistance, including care delivered by trusted family members or other qualified individuals.

Authorities contend that the program’s structure can become vulnerable when individuals intentionally manipulate billing procedures for personal financial gain.

The investigation is part of a broader federal and state push to identify and prosecute fraud involving Medicare and Medicaid. Officials say they intend to increase efforts to prevent individuals and businesses from improperly profiting from government health care programs.

At this point, authorities have not publicly released the names of all 19 defendants or provided a complete breakdown of the allegations against each individual. Officials also have not detailed the potential penalties the defendants could face if they are ultimately convicted.

The charges are allegations, and all defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty in court.
