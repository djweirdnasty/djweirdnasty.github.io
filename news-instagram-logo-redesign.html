Instagram Gives Its Logo a Fresh Look — But Users Are Already Roasting the Redesign

National | Tech

Published: Wednesday, August 13, 2026

Instagram is changing its look for the first time in years, but the reaction from users has been anything but unanimous.

The Meta-owned platform introduced a redesigned Instagram wordmark Thursday, marking the first major update to the lettering displayed across the app since 2016.

Instagram says the goal was to create a cleaner, more contemporary design while keeping elements connected to the platform’s original visual identity.

Instagram chief Adam Mosseri announced the updated wordmark through his social media accounts.

The New Wordmark

The redesign moves away from the fully handwritten appearance users have become accustomed to. The new lettering combines more traditional printed forms with elements of the familiar script style.

According to Instagram, the update was designed to maintain the personality of the brand while making the wordmark feel more current.

The app’s recognizable gradient camera icon, however, was not part of this particular redesign.

Mixed Reactions Online

It didn’t take long for social media users to begin dissecting the new design.

One of the biggest complaints involves the way certain letters appear in the new wordmark. Some viewers say the redesigned lettering makes the brand name look like “Instagzam” or other humorous variations.

Others joked that the logo appears as though it was written by a young child, while some commenters questioned whether the new lettering was as easy to read as Instagram intended.

The criticism quickly became part of the conversation surrounding the launch, with users sharing their own interpretations of the redesigned wordmark.

Supporters of the Change

Not everyone is against the change.

Social media consultant Matt Navarra acknowledged the criticism while saying he personally liked the redesign. He described the updated wordmark as fresh and modern while noting that some people were interpreting the lettering differently.

That split reaction highlights one of the biggest challenges with a major brand redesign: a change that feels polished and contemporary to designers can look strange or confusing to longtime users.

Bigger Platform Complaints

Beyond jokes about the logo, some users used the announcement to bring up issues they believe deserve more attention.

Critics questioned why the company was focusing on branding when creators and businesses continue to report problems with account access, customer support and automated systems.

Others called for better tools to deal with bots and artificial-intelligence-generated content.

One large Instagram account argued that creators need clearer explanations and better ways to recover accounts when they lose access.

The comments demonstrate that the logo announcement became more than a discussion about typography. For some users, it became an opportunity to voice broader frustrations with the platform.

Brand History

Instagram’s branding has remained highly recognizable for years, making any change to the wordmark likely to attract attention.

The company last made a major visual shift in 2016, when it introduced the colorful gradient camera icon that became synonymous with the platform.

This latest update focuses specifically on the “Instagram” lettering rather than replacing the familiar app icon.

Rollout

Instagram says the redesigned wordmark will gradually become available around the world during the remainder of 2026 and beyond.

The response so far appears divided.

Some Instagram users believe the new design gives the platform a more modern appearance and better reflects how the company has evolved.

Others think the previous lettering was more recognizable and easier to read.

And, of course, plenty of users are simply having fun with it.

For now, the redesigned wordmark has already accomplished one thing: it got people talking.

Whether users eventually embrace the new look or continue calling it “Instagzam” may become clear as the design rolls out more broadly.
