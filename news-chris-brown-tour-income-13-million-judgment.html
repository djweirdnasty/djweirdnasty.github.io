Chris Brown Faces Growing Legal Pressure as Former Housekeeper Targets Tour Income in $13.8 Million Judgment Fight

Music News

Published: Friday, August 21, 2026

Chris Brown’s legal problems surrounding a 2020 dog attack are continuing to follow him while he remains on the road with Usher.

The singer is fighting an aggressive collection effort from Maria Avila, his former housekeeper, after a Los Angeles jury found Brown and his company, Black Pyramid LLC, liable in connection with injuries Avila suffered when she was attacked by a large guard dog at Brown’s Tarzana, California, property.

Avila was awarded $12.9 million in damages. Her sister, Patricia Avila, was awarded another $885,000 for emotional distress, while Maria’s husband received $50,000. Altogether, the awards total approximately $13.8 million.

Now, the dispute has moved beyond the original trial.

Avila’s legal team wants to locate and potentially intercept money connected to Brown’s entertainment career, including income associated with his ongoing stadium tour with Usher. Brown has pushed back against those efforts while simultaneously asking the court to overturn the verdict and give him a new trial.

How the Dispute Started

The case dates back to December 2020, when Avila was working at Brown’s Tarzana residence.

According to the lawsuit, Avila was performing housekeeping duties at the property when she encountered Hades, a large Caucasian Shepherd that Brown’s team used as a guard dog.

Avila suffered severe injuries during the attack, including damage to her face and arm. Reporting on the case says she required extensive medical treatment, including skin grafts, and has described lasting physical and emotional consequences from the incident.

The circumstances surrounding the dogs became a major point of disagreement during the trial.

Brown testified that he had warned the housekeepers about the animals and said they should not enter certain areas without security personnel. The Avilas disputed that account.

Brown also maintained that the dog was being used as part of his security operation rather than being a personal household pet.

The jury ultimately rejected the defense’s position and found Brown and Black Pyramid LLC liable.

Jury Awards Avila Nearly $13 Million

After a two-week civil trial, jurors reached their decision on June 30.

Maria Avila received $12.9 million, with the award covering damages associated with the injuries and their consequences.

Her sister Patricia, who was present during the incident, received $885,000 for emotional distress. Maria’s husband was awarded $50,000.

The verdict represented a significant financial judgment against Brown.

But winning a jury verdict and actually collecting the money are two different stages of a civil case.

That distinction has become central to what is happening now.

Avila Looks Toward Brown’s Music Income

After the verdict, Avila’s attorneys began pursuing ways to collect the judgment.

According to reports, her legal team argued that Brown had not made efforts to satisfy the award and asked the court for access to information about his finances.

One of the biggest targets is Brown’s current touring income.

Brown is currently performing with Usher as part of The R&B Tour, a major stadium run that began in June and is scheduled to continue through the end of the year.

Avila’s lawyers have argued that the tour represents a significant source of revenue that could potentially be used to satisfy the judgment. They have also sought information concerning other forms of entertainment income.

The collection effort could potentially involve several types of revenue, depending on what the court ultimately permits.

Those sources could include:

  • Concert-related payments
  • Royalties
  • Licensing revenue
  • Merchandise income
  • Sponsorship payments
  • Other entertainment-related earnings

However, Avila’s request should not be confused with a final order automatically taking Brown’s tour money.

The reported court filings concern efforts to obtain financial information and use legal collection mechanisms against Brown’s income. Brown is opposing those efforts.

Brown Pushes Back

Brown’s legal team has not simply accepted the judgment.

The singer and Black Pyramid LLC have taken steps to challenge the verdict and seek a new trial.

His attorneys have raised several objections to the original proceedings, including claims involving the conduct of the trial, the jury, the amount of damages and evidence presented to jurors.

More recently, Brown’s lawyers focused on testimony concerning his past.

During questioning at trial, Brown acknowledged that he was a convicted felon, a response that referenced his 2009 felony assault conviction involving Rihanna.

Brown’s attorneys now argue that bringing that conviction before the jury was prejudicial and unrelated to the central question of whether Brown was responsible for the 2020 dog attack.

His lawyers are asking for the verdict to be thrown out and for the case to be heard again before a different jury.

Avila’s attorneys have rejected that argument and maintain that the evidence supporting the verdict was strong.

The Timing Could Be Important

The legal fight comes while Brown is performing one of the biggest tours of his career.

That creates an unusual situation in which the singer’s active entertainment schedule is occurring at the same time his former housekeeper is attempting to enforce a multimillion-dollar civil judgment.

The collection issue could therefore become increasingly important as the tour continues.

If the judgment remains outstanding, Avila can continue pursuing legally available collection remedies. At the same time, Brown can continue challenging the underlying verdict and contesting the scope of the collection efforts.

The outcome of those competing legal strategies could determine whether Brown’s entertainment income remains under his control or becomes subject to additional court-directed collection measures.

Why the Tour Matters

For entertainers, income can be structured differently from a traditional employee’s paycheck.

A major concert tour can involve promoters, management companies, production companies, record labels, publishers, booking agencies and other business entities.

That means determining exactly where an artist’s money is held, who owes the artist money and which payments can legally be reached by a creditor can become a complicated process.

That appears to be why Avila’s attorneys are seeking financial information rather than simply demanding a single payment from Brown.

The goal is reportedly to identify potential sources of revenue that could be used to satisfy the judgment.

Brown’s Legal Challenge Could Change the Situation

Brown’s request for a new trial adds another layer to the dispute.

A successful challenge could potentially change or eliminate the judgment. But until a court actually grants the requested relief, the existing judgment remains an important part of the collection dispute.

A hearing on Brown’s new-trial request has been reported for September 22, 2026.

That date could become an important moment in determining what happens next.

If Brown succeeds in getting a new trial or otherwise overturning the judgment, Avila’s collection position could change dramatically.

If Brown’s challenge fails, Avila could continue pursuing collection of the existing award.

The Bigger Picture

The case illustrates an important distinction in celebrity lawsuits: a jury award does not necessarily mean the money immediately changes hands.

Once a plaintiff wins a substantial civil judgment, the dispute can enter a separate phase involving financial disclosures, collection orders and attempts to identify assets or income.

That’s where the Brown case now stands.

Avila has already secured a substantial jury verdict. Her legal team is now focused on turning that verdict into an actual payment.

Brown, meanwhile, is attempting to prevent the judgment from becoming final while defending his income and challenging the trial itself.

With the singer continuing his tour and another court hearing approaching, the case is far from finished.

What Happens Next?

The next major developments could involve:

  1. Brown’s new-trial request — The court will consider his arguments that the verdict should be overturned.
  2. Avila’s collection efforts — Her attorneys can continue pursuing information about Brown’s finances and potential sources of income.
  3. Brown’s tour earnings — The court could ultimately have to address whether particular tour-related payments can legally be reached to satisfy the judgment.
  4. The outstanding judgment — Unless the verdict is overturned, reduced, settled or otherwise resolved, Brown remains responsible for the awards entered against him.

For now, Chris Brown’s tour with Usher continues while his lawyers and Avila’s legal team battle over what happens to the money generated during that run.

The original dog-attack lawsuit may have ended with a jury verdict, but the fight over the financial consequences is becoming its own legal battle.
