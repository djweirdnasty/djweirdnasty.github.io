Arkansas Woman Sentenced to 20 Years After Shooting Ex-Boyfriend 17 Times

National | Crime

Published: Sunday, August 23, 2026

Rebecca Jane Mryglod, 38, has been sentenced to 20 years in an Arkansas state prison after admitting that she shot her former boyfriend repeatedly inside his home in a November 2024 attack that left him critically injured but alive.

Mryglod pleaded guilty to first-degree domestic battery in Cleburne County and received a 240-month sentence in the Arkansas Department of Correction. Court records indicate that she had already received credit for 648 days served.

The case stems from a shooting on Nov. 8, 2024, at a residence in Drasco, Arkansas, where Mryglod had been living with her former boyfriend.

According to investigative records, Mryglod told authorities that she had returned to the home after the couple’s relationship ended because she wanted to help him deal with what she described as his “spiritual demons.”

That arrangement ultimately ended in a violent confrontation.

Shooting Followed Morning Routine

Investigators said Mryglod had purchased a .22-caliber handgun only days before the shooting. She reportedly told detectives that she had practiced firing the weapon before the incident.

On the morning of Nov. 8, Mryglod got up early and helped her daughter leave for school. After returning to the house, she encountered her former boyfriend sitting on a couch.

According to her account to investigators, she asked him whether he intended to go to work.

As he began getting up, Mryglod said she turned, raised the handgun and started firing.

The victim was struck more than 17 times, according to investigative and court records. The shooting caused life-threatening injuries and required extensive medical treatment.

The victim later told investigators that the gunfire began as he was standing up from the couch. He also alleged that Mryglod moved toward the door before firing again at close range.

She Called 911 After the Attack

Following the shooting, Mryglod left the residence and contacted emergency services herself.

The 911 caller was reportedly crying and asking for an ambulance before telling dispatchers that she had shot someone.

When deputies arrived, they found the victim inside the home suffering from numerous gunshot wounds. Investigators documented blood inside the residence as well as around the entrance and front steps.

Mryglod was outside when officers arrived. She was reportedly crying heavily and breathing rapidly, with blood visible on her hands.

She identified the injured man as her boyfriend and acknowledged shooting him.

Mryglod also repeatedly asked whether he was going to die. According to the incident report, a responding deputy told her that he could hear the victim making noise, which was considered an encouraging sign.

Investigators later recovered a black KelTec .22-caliber handgun from nearby vegetation. The weapon’s magazine was empty and its slide was locked open.

Victim Faced Serious Medical Complications

The shooting left the victim in critical condition.

Among his injuries was a gunshot wound that damaged his colon. The resulting infection required additional surgery as doctors worked to treat the complications caused by the shooting.

Despite the severity of his injuries, he survived.

During a December 2024 interview with investigators, the victim provided his account of what happened inside the home. He said Mryglod opened fire as he got up from the couch.

He also told detectives that he had previously accompanied Mryglod when she purchased the firearm and had gone target shooting with her.

That detail became part of the investigative record as authorities reconstructed the events leading up to the attack.

Mryglod Described Her Frustration to Detectives

During questioning, Mryglod offered investigators an explanation for the shooting that centered on her frustration with her former boyfriend’s spiritual struggles.

She said she had moved back into the home because she believed she could help him deal with those problems. According to the affidavit, however, she eventually became tired of the situation.

Investigators recorded Mryglod as saying she would rather her former boyfriend be dead and herself be incarcerated than continue dealing with what she described as his spiritual difficulties.

She also acknowledged that she had obtained the firearm several days before the shooting and had practiced using it.

Original Charges Were More Serious

Mryglod initially faced several charges stemming from the incident.

Prosecutors charged her with attempted first-degree murder, first-degree domestic battery and simultaneous possession of drugs and firearms.

The case ultimately ended with a plea agreement.

Rather than proceeding to trial on the original charges, Mryglod pleaded guilty to first-degree domestic battery. She was subsequently sentenced to 240 months, or 20 years, in the Arkansas Department of Correction.

The sentence brings a legal conclusion to a case that began with a shooting that could have resulted in a fatality.

A Case That Began With a Troubled Relationship

The circumstances surrounding the shooting illustrate how quickly an already strained relationship can escalate into serious violence.

Mryglod and the victim had previously been romantically involved and had broken up before she moved back into the residence. Her stated reason for returning was to help him with personal and spiritual difficulties.

According to investigators, that situation deteriorated before the November 2024 shooting.

The victim survived the attack despite suffering an extraordinary number of gunshot wounds and undergoing significant medical treatment.

Mryglod’s decision to call 911 immediately afterward also became an important part of the initial police response. She remained at the scene and acknowledged her involvement when deputies arrived.

Twenty-Year Sentence Ends Case

More than a year and a half after the shooting, Mryglod’s guilty plea resulted in a 20-year prison sentence.

Her case serves as another example of the serious criminal consequences that can follow domestic violence involving firearms. What began as an attempt, according to Mryglod’s account, to help a former partner ultimately resulted in a shooting, critical injuries and a lengthy prison term.

The victim survived, but the physical consequences included major injuries and complications requiring additional medical care.

Mryglod will now serve her sentence in the Arkansas Department of Correction after pleading guilty to first-degree domestic battery.
