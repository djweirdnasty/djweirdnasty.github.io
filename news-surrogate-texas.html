Surrogate Refuses Abortion Request, Gives Birth to Baby With Serious Heart Defect in Texas

National | Health & Law

Published: Wednesday, August 13, 2026

A surrogate mother has delivered a baby in Texas after reportedly declining a request from the child’s intended parents to terminate the pregnancy following a prenatal diagnosis of a serious heart condition.

McKenna West was around 20 weeks pregnant when doctors diagnosed the unborn child with hypoplastic left heart syndrome, a congenital condition that affects the development of the heart’s left side.

The Dispute

According to the Texas attorney general’s office, West was asked by the baby’s intended parents to have an abortion after they learned about the diagnosis. She reportedly refused and later traveled to Texas while continuing the pregnancy.

The situation eventually became a legal matter, with Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton’s office becoming involved. Officials notified UT Southwestern Medical Center and Children’s Medical Center of Dallas that the newborn should receive medically necessary treatment after delivery.

Baby Gabriel’s Arrival

The baby, Gabriel, was born Wednesday and was subsequently taken to a neonatal intensive care unit, according to reports. Doctors are expected to determine the appropriate treatment for his heart condition, which can require major surgery shortly after birth.

The legal dispute reportedly centered in part on whether the intended parents would consent to the surgery. Paxton’s office said the parents had not agreed to authorize the potentially lifesaving procedure, prompting the state to seek legal protections for the infant.

State Response

Paxton said the state would continue working to ensure Gabriel receives the treatment doctors determine he needs.

“Baby Gabriel deserves a chance at life,” Paxton said in a statement, adding that his office would use the legal authority available to protect the newborn’s access to medical care.

The hospital where Gabriel is expected to receive treatment has reportedly achieved a 100% success rate for the procedure in question.

Broader Questions

The case has drawn national attention because it raises complicated questions involving surrogacy agreements, medical decision-making and the rights of intended parents and surrogates when a serious fetal condition is discovered during pregnancy.

For now, Gabriel remains under medical care as doctors assess his condition and prepare for the treatment that could be necessary to address the congenital heart defect.
