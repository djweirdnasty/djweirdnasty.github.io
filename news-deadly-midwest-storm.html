Deadly Midwest Storms Leave 3 Dead and Nearly 800,000 Without Power

National News

Published: Wednesday, August 12, 2026

A powerful wave of severe weather swept through parts of the Midwest Tuesday, leaving at least three people dead, damaging homes and businesses, flooding communities and knocking out electricity for hundreds of thousands of customers.

Officials reported two storm-related deaths in Indiana and another in Ohio as powerful winds, heavy rain and tornadoes moved across the region. Some areas experienced wind gusts greater than 80 mph, creating dangerous conditions and widespread destruction.

In Portage, Indiana, a home was hit by an explosion and fire around midday. Emergency crews faced major obstacles reaching the area because trees and power lines had fallen across roads throughout the community.

Another death occurred in Jennings County, Indiana, where a 4-year-old boy was killed after a tree fell onto his home in Geneva Township. Local authorities said the severe weather and strong winds were believed to have contributed to the incident.

Ohio officials also reported a storm-related death in Roseville. Gov. Mike DeWine later declared a state of emergency in portions of the state as flooding forced evacuations and prompted water rescues.

Columbus was among the communities hit particularly hard. Mayor Andrew Ginther described conditions across the city as resembling a disaster zone, with widespread damage, fallen trees and electrical outages. He warned residents that repairs could take several days and urged people to prepare for continued disruptions.

The storms also caused major problems for the region's power grid. Nearly 800,000 customers across Illinois, Indiana, Ohio and Kentucky were without electricity Wednesday morning. Ohio and Indiana accounted for a large portion of the reported outages.

Utility crews were deployed throughout the affected areas, with additional workers brought in from other states to help restore service. AEP reported that the Columbus area experienced wind gusts exceeding 80 mph and said thousands of workers were involved in recovery efforts.

Northern Illinois also experienced tornado activity. The National Weather Service confirmed two tornadoes near Chicago, including one in southern Cook County and another in eastern Will County. Both produced damage consistent with EF1-strength tornadoes.

In Portage, the storm damage was so extensive that police officers had difficulty reaching people who needed help. Some officers reportedly left their vehicles and traveled on foot after fallen trees and utility lines made roads nearly impossible to navigate.

Severe winds caused additional structural damage throughout the region. Videos from affected communities showed vehicles being pushed or overturned, damaged roofs and trees scattered across roads and properties.

In Frankfort, Illinois, a family lost its home to a fire during the storm. The homeowner said she was inside with her elderly mother when they heard a loud explosion. Officials believe lightning or electrical equipment damaged during the storm may have started the fire.

The homeowner said she eventually noticed smoke inside the house and quickly realized she and her mother needed to leave. The residence was later considered a total loss.

Flooding created another serious threat in Ohio. Emergency officials reported evacuations and water rescues in parts of Perry and Muskingum counties after heavy rainfall caused water levels to rise.

Severe weather also affected a correctional facility in Grafton, Ohio. Sixteen inmates were taken to hospitals after lightning struck as prisoners were returning from an evening meal. One person was transported by medical helicopter.

Forecasters had warned that a frontal boundary moving through the north-central United States could produce dangerous thunderstorms across parts of the Midwest and Ohio Valley. Some areas faced the possibility of extremely powerful winds approaching 100 mph.

With additional storms possible, officials are urging residents to remain alert, avoid flooded roads, stay away from downed power lines and prepare for potentially extended power outages.

Communities across the region now face a difficult cleanup and recovery effort as crews work to clear roads, repair electrical infrastructure, assess damaged buildings and assist residents affected by the severe weather.

Sources: NBC News, National Weather Service, local emergency officials and utility providers.
