NBA YoungBoy Says He May Never Return to America After South Korea Trip

Music | Hip-Hop

Published: Wednesday, August 12, 2026

NBA YoungBoy is giving fans a glimpse into what his future could look like after telling Funny Marco that he does not see himself returning to the United States.

The rapper, born Kentrell DeSean Gaulden, discussed his experience in South Korea during an episode of Funny Marco's "Open Thoughts" interview series.

The conversation took place while the two were in South Korea, which YoungBoy said was also his first time visiting the country. The Baton Rouge native appeared to enjoy the experience and highlighted several things he liked about being there.

Among them were the country's atmosphere, cleanliness and what he described as a strong sense of safety.

For YoungBoy, the appeal also appears to be the distance from his usual environment. When asked what he liked about South Korea, he initially described it as being "out the way."

The discussion eventually turned toward whether he could imagine returning to the United States.

YoungBoy's answer was blunt.

"Never in life."

The rapper explained that international travel was not something he experienced much growing up. Now that he has the opportunity to see different parts of the world, he said he does not have an issue with staying away from home.

His children remain an important consideration, however. YoungBoy acknowledged that being away from them is significant but pointed to air travel as a way to maintain that connection.

The comments come at an interesting point in YoungBoy's career.

Despite being only 26, the rapper has already built an extensive catalog and has been one of the most active artists in hip-hop over the past decade.

During the interview, YoungBoy indicated that his music career may eventually wind down. He said he expects to release perhaps one or two more albums and believes he will only embark on one additional tour.

That would represent a major change for an artist known for releasing music at a relentless pace.

YoungBoy first gained widespread attention through a series of mixtapes released during his teenage years. His popularity continued to grow as he accumulated Billboard chart appearances and developed a massive online following.

His 2021 album "Sincerely, Kentrell" reached No. 1 on the Billboard 200, adding another major accomplishment to his résumé.

His career has also been accompanied by significant legal troubles.

YoungBoy faced federal charges stemming from a Louisiana investigation involving firearms and alleged drug-related activity. He later faced another federal case in Los Angeles.

He was ultimately acquitted in the California case but received a 23-month sentence in connection with the Louisiana case, followed by probation.

After spending more than three years under federal custody and home confinement, YoungBoy was released in April 2025. He received a presidential pardon from Donald Trump two months later.

Now, the rapper appears to be looking toward a different kind of future.

Whether YoungBoy actually makes South Korea his permanent home remains uncertain. His comments, however, make it clear that he is enjoying life outside the United States and is open to spending considerably more time abroad.

If he follows through on his plans to scale back his music career, his decision to live overseas could mark another unexpected chapter in the career of one of hip-hop's most prolific young artists.
