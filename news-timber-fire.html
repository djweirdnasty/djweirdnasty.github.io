Timber Fire Nears 3,800 Acres as Big Sur Evacuations and Highway 1 Shutdown Continue

National | Wildfires

Published: Wednesday, August 12, 2026

The Timber Fire has expanded to 3,774 acres in Monterey County as firefighters continue battling flames across the steep and rugged terrain surrounding Big Sur.

CAL FIRE reported Wednesday that the fire remains 5% contained, with 503 structures currently threatened. No structures have been reported damaged or destroyed, and officials have not reported any civilian or firefighter injuries or deaths.

Firefighters faced an additional challenge Wednesday when thunderstorms moved over the area. Lightning activity near the fire forced crews to temporarily retreat from portions of the fire line and move to safer locations.

Crews are expected to reassess the situation and resume suppression work once the weather improves and conditions are considered safe.

Fire activity increased near Partington Ridge and Coast Ridge Road, where firefighters have been working to slow the flames and protect nearby homes.

On the western side of the fire, ground crews and bulldozer operators have been building containment lines toward Highway 1. Firefighters have also been checking homes for hot spots and conducting patrols around properties in threatened areas.

Burning debris and embers along Coast Ridge Road sparked several spot fires, but crews were able to quickly suppress them.

Firefighters on the northern flank have been constructing additional lines toward Highway 1 and the Big Sur River. The eastern portion of the fire remains active in wilderness areas, where steep terrain and dense vegetation continue to make access difficult.

A total of 904 personnel are assigned to the Timber Fire response. The operation includes engines, hand crews, bulldozers, helicopters and water tenders, with aircraft also assisting when weather conditions allow.

Preventing the fire from crossing Highway 1 and protecting nearby homes remain major priorities for firefighters.

The wildfire has grown significantly since it began Saturday night. After initially covering a much smaller area, the fire expanded rapidly over several days and has now reached nearly 3,800 acres.

A roughly 10-mile stretch of Highway 1 remains closed through Big Sur because of the wildfire and evacuation activity.

The closure runs from the area near Fernwood Resort to the Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park Vista Point. Officials are asking motorists to avoid the area and follow directions from emergency personnel.

Drivers are also being reminded to keep roads clear for firefighters, emergency responders and residents leaving evacuation zones.

Evacuation orders remain in effect for Monterey County zones MRY-F023, MRY-F025, MRY-F026, MRY-F027 and MRY-F028-A.

Evacuation warnings remain active for zones MRY-F021-B, MRY-F022, MRY-F024, MRY-F028-B and MRY-F029.

Residents in areas under evacuation orders are being urged to leave immediately and bring essential items, including medications, important documents, emergency supplies and pets.

People in evacuation warning areas should be prepared to leave quickly if conditions worsen or officials issue additional evacuation orders.

Carmel Middle School is serving as a shelter and temporary assistance center for people displaced by the fire.

The shelter is providing food, water, blankets, cots and other basic necessities. Monterey County and American Red Cross personnel are also available to help evacuees find additional resources.

Residents evacuating with pets are encouraged to bring food, medications, leashes, carriers and other supplies needed for their animals.

Weather remains a significant concern for firefighting crews. Additional thunderstorms are possible through Thursday morning, bringing the potential for lightning, strong winds and rapidly changing fire conditions.

Those weather conditions could temporarily limit firefighting operations and make the fire more difficult to predict.

Crews continue working to strengthen containment lines, protect homes and prevent the wildfire from advancing farther along the Highway 1 corridor.

The cause of the Timber Fire remains under investigation.
