Remy Ma Fires Back at Claressa Shields With Savage “Clearance Rack” Diss on “Wanna Go”

Music | Hip-Hop

Published: Friday, August 14, 2026

Remy Ma appears to be answering Claressa Shields with a new diss track, and she isn’t exactly being subtle.

The Bronx rapper recently released “Wanna Go,” a record that appears to address Shields following comments the professional boxer made about her relationship with Papoose.

Rather than relying solely on direct name-calling, Remy builds the song around the idea of new versus used, bringing fashion, resale and shopping references into the lyrics.

One of the most talked-about moments comes through Remy’s wordplay involving “Clarence” and “clearance.” Remy has previously referred to Shields as “Clarence,” and the new track appears to connect that nickname with repeated references to a “clearance rack.”

The timing of the song has made the lyrics even more noticeable.

Shields recently appeared on Cam Newton’s “Funky Friday,” where the conversation turned toward the criticism surrounding her relationship with Papoose. During the discussion, Shields suggested that some of the women criticizing the relationship were upset because Papoose was not actually “downgrading.”

That comment appears to have provided Remy with plenty of ammunition.

On “Wanna Go,” Remy repeatedly contrasts things that are brand new with items that have already been owned or are being purchased from discounted sections of a store. The messaging appears designed to challenge the idea that Shields represents an upgrade.

The song arrives during an already complicated public dispute between Remy, Papoose and Shields.

Remy and Papoose were together for more than a decade and share a daughter. Their relationship eventually became the subject of public controversy, with accusations being exchanged as their marriage broke down. Papoose filed for divorce in 2025, and his relationship with Shields later became public.

The situation escalated again following Remy’s release of “W.Y.F.L.,” a freestyle containing several pointed shots at Papoose.

Papoose’s attorney reportedly sent a cease-and-desist letter to Remy’s legal team in April, demanding that the song be removed and objecting to what his side described as insulting and damaging statements.

Remy did not appear to let the legal dispute stop her from performing the record. She has continued referencing the situation publicly, including during performances.

Now “Wanna Go” adds another layer to the ongoing feud.

The song doesn’t need to repeatedly mention Shields by name for the intended message to be understood. Between the “Clarence” and “clearance” wordplay and the repeated references to resale and secondhand merchandise, Remy makes it clear that the track is connected to the current drama.

For now, the public back-and-forth between the three continues to generate headlines, and Remy’s latest musical response suggests she still has plenty more to say.

The claims and accusations made by the people involved remain disputed unless and until they are established through the legal process.

The “Knuck If You Buck” Connection

Remy also rapped over the instrumental for Crime Mob’s “Knuck If You Buck” on “Wanna Go.” The choice was especially noticeable because it came after Crime Mob’s Diamond had publicly expressed frustration with Claressa Shields, claiming she had been blocked from performing the track as Shields walked to the ring for an upcoming match. Using the same instrumental appeared to tie Remy’s diss back into the larger public back-and-forth.
