Luigi Mangione Admits Role in Killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO in Federal Guilty Plea

National | Crime

Published: Friday, August 14, 2026

Luigi Mangione pleaded guilty Friday to federal charges connected to the 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson, formally acknowledging in court that he planned the attack and shot the executive in Manhattan.

Mangione, 28, entered his plea in federal court in Manhattan while members of Thompson’s family watched from the courtroom. During his statement to the judge, Mangione described how he contacted UnitedHealthcare while pretending to be a potential investor in an effort to learn where the company’s investor conference would take place.

He told the court that his purpose was to locate Thompson and that he ultimately shot him on Dec. 4, 2024.

Mangione also acknowledged that he understood the shooting could cause Thompson to fear for his life and that he knew his actions were illegal.

According to prosecutors, Mangione’s motivation was connected to his experiences dealing with the U.S. health care system and years of serious back pain. Investigators have previously said he had no personal relationship with Thompson and was not a UnitedHealthcare customer.

Prosecutors Seeking Life Sentence

Mangione pleaded guilty to two federal stalking offenses involving interstate activity that resulted in Thompson’s death. The charges carry a maximum sentence of life in federal prison.

Federal prosecutors initially indicated that sentencing guidelines could place the recommended range around 24 to 30 years. However, the U.S. Attorney’s Office later made clear that it intends to ask the judge for the maximum sentence.

Mangione is scheduled to be sentenced on Dec. 18. The final punishment will be determined by the judge.

U.S. Attorney James McDonald emphasized that there was no negotiated plea agreement. He described the plea as an “open” guilty plea, meaning prosecutors did not agree to concessions in exchange for Mangione admitting guilt.

State Murder Case Still Pending

The federal plea does not automatically end the separate criminal case against Mangione in New York state court.

He continues to face a second-degree murder charge and weapons-related charges in connection with Thompson’s death. Jury selection in that case is currently scheduled to begin Sept. 8.

Mangione’s attorneys say they intend to challenge the state prosecution under double jeopardy protections, arguing that pursuing the state case after the federal proceedings could improperly subject him to prosecution for the same conduct.

That legal dispute could potentially delay the state trial and may ultimately require review by an appeals court.

Thompson’s Family Speaks

Thompson’s relatives attended Friday’s federal hearing. His family later said the guilty plea represented an important step toward accountability, while emphasizing that it could never undo the loss of the father and husband.

The family has largely avoided public attention since Thompson was killed. Authorities have also dealt with threats directed toward Thompson’s relatives amid the intense public attention surrounding Mangione.

What Happened in 2024

Thompson was shot outside a Manhattan hotel on Dec. 4, 2024, while he was in New York for a UnitedHealthcare investor conference.

Mangione was arrested five days later at a McDonald’s restaurant in Altoona, Pennsylvania.

Investigators have said they recovered a notebook containing writings about the health insurance industry as well as other evidence they believe connected Mangione to the shooting. Authorities also recovered shell casings at the crime scene bearing words associated with Mangione’s alleged grievances against the insurance industry.

Mangione’s attorneys previously attempted to prevent the notebook from being used as evidence, arguing that it had been obtained through an unlawful search. Judges in both cases rejected those challenges.

Mangione had previously faced a federal firearms charge that could have exposed him to the death penalty, but that charge was dismissed earlier this year.

His guilty plea now moves the federal case toward sentencing while his legal team prepares to fight the separate state prosecution.
