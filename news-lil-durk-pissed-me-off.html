Lil Durk’s “Pissed Me Off” Music Video Allowed as Evidence in Federal Trial

Music | Hip-Hop

Published: Friday, August 14, 2026

CHICAGO — A federal judge has ruled that prosecutors will be allowed to present Lil Durk’s 2021 song “Pissed Me Off” and its accompanying music video to jurors when the rapper goes to trial on federal murder-for-hire charges.

U.S. District Judge Michael Fitzgerald reportedly made the decision Thursday, Aug. 13, siding with prosecutors who argued that the song could help establish Durk’s alleged motive and intent.

Durk, whose legal name is Durk Banks, has pleaded not guilty to allegations that he helped finance an attempted killing targeting rapper Quando Rondo. Authorities allege the shooting was connected to the 2020 death of Durk’s longtime associate and fellow rapper King Von, whose real name was Dayvon Bennett.

The 2022 attack took place in Los Angeles. Rondo survived the shooting, but his cousin, Saviay’a “Lul Pab” Robinson, was killed.

Prosecutors Point to Song Lyrics

Federal prosecutors have focused on portions of “Pissed Me Off” that reference paying for guns, vehicles and other expenses before mentioning people who invoke Von’s name.

The government contends that those lyrics, recorded months before the Los Angeles shooting, provide insight into Durk’s alleged willingness to financially support retaliation.

Prosecutors are expected to use a cooperating witness connected to the music video to provide additional context surrounding the song and some of its imagery.

Defense Says Lyrics Are Being Taken Literally

Durk’s attorneys have pushed back against the government’s interpretation, arguing that the song reflects several painful experiences in the rapper’s life rather than describing a specific criminal plan.

Defense attorney Marissa Goldberg reportedly pointed to the death of Durk’s older brother, Dontay “DThang” Banks Jr., who was killed in 2021. She argued that using the song to suggest Durk was admitting or foreshadowing a murder conspiracy could unfairly influence the jury.

The defense has also raised concerns that allowing the song into evidence could expose jurors to allegations that have been separated from the upcoming murder-for-hire case.

Judge Fitzgerald nevertheless determined that the prosecution could introduce the song and video. However, the ruling does not prevent the defense from challenging how prosecutors characterize the material.

The judge could also provide jurors with instructions limiting how they are allowed to use the song when evaluating the government’s case.

Trial Expected to Begin Soon

Durk was arrested in October 2024 and has maintained his innocence.

Jury selection is expected to begin Aug. 20, with opening statements potentially following the next day.

The case is likely to spark another debate over the role of rap lyrics in criminal trials, with prosecutors portraying “Pissed Me Off” as evidence relevant to motive while the defense argues that artistic expression should not automatically be treated as a factual admission of criminal conduct.

The ultimate significance of the song will be determined by the jury after hearing the prosecution’s evidence and the defense’s arguments.
