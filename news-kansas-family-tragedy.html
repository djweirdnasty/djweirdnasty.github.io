Kansas Family Tragedy: Authorities Reveal Man's Criminal History and Alleged Family Murder-Suicide Plan

National | Crime

Published: Tuesday, August 18, 2026

New information is emerging about the Winfield, Kansas, family whose deaths shocked the community, including records detailing the criminal history of Ronald H. Williams Sr. and investigators' findings about what led to the killings.

Williams, 53, his wife, Kelly George, 44, and their four children were found dead inside their home on Aug. 11. Investigators have described the deaths as a murder-suicide and say evidence indicates that Williams and George were both involved in a plan to kill the children before taking their own lives.

The children were identified as Carol Williams, 9; Ronald Williams Jr., 7; Sarah Williams, 5; and Kelly Magee-Williams, 3.

Williams Had a Lengthy Criminal Record

Court records reviewed by authorities show that Williams had a history of criminal cases dating back decades. Records indicate he had used several names, including Freddie Williams, Laron Williams and Jerome Williams.

Williams was convicted in Kansas in 1998 of attempted indecent liberties with a child involving a 15-year-old girl. The Kansas Bureau of Investigation's registry records indicate the underlying offense occurred in 1997.

His criminal record also included allegations and charges involving drug offenses, battery, assault, burglary, kidnapping, attempted rape and an alleged assault involving a law enforcement officer. The cases reportedly span multiple Kansas counties and occurred over a period stretching from the mid-1990s through 2024.

911 Call Preceded Discovery of Six Bodies

The deaths were discovered after Williams reportedly called 911 at approximately 8:50 a.m. on Aug. 11.

According to authorities, the caller identified himself as Williams and told dispatchers that he had killed his family and planned to kill himself.

Winfield police responded to the residence and reportedly heard a gunshot after arriving. Officers established a perimeter before entering the property, where they discovered all six members of the household deceased.

Investigators later identified a Taurus .38 Special revolver recovered at the scene as the suspected weapon. Authorities said Williams had purchased the firearm in 2025.

Investigators Say Both Parents Were Involved

Several days after the deaths, the Kansas Bureau of Investigation announced that evidence pointed to both Williams and George having a role in the planning.

Investigators said the couple appeared to have jointly participated in a premeditated plan involving the deaths of their children and themselves. Officials have not publicly released the specific evidence supporting that conclusion, saying the investigation remains active.

Authorities also said the couple displayed increasingly unusual and erratic behavior before the tragedy.

Warning Signs Had Been Reported

Police records and local reports indicate that concerns about Williams' behavior had surfaced repeatedly.

In 2024, a woman reportedly told police that Williams pointed a gun at her while she was walking her dog. Williams allegedly claimed he was attempting to protect himself from the animal.

In 2025, he was also accused of pointing a firearm at a FedEx driver who had briefly used his driveway to turn around.

Williams and George reportedly made numerous complaints to authorities themselves, including allegations involving mail interference, conspiracies and other perceived threats.

A police officer who documented interactions with the couple reportedly expressed concerns about their behavior and advised officers to exercise caution.

Williams' mother also requested a welfare check in April 2025. During that encounter, Williams reportedly told police he was "cutting people out of my life."

A neighbor's doorbell camera allegedly captured Williams yelling outside the residence on Aug. 7, just four days before the family was found dead.

Investigation Continues

The KBI and Winfield Police Department continue to investigate the circumstances surrounding the deaths. Authorities have said they do not believe the public faces an ongoing threat.
