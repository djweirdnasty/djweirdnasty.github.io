14 Charged in Alleged Cocaine Operation Connected to Penn State Fraternities

National | Crime

Published: Tuesday, August 18, 2026

Fourteen people are facing criminal charges in connection with an alleged cocaine-trafficking operation that investigators say involved current and former Penn State students and two off-campus fraternities.

Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday announced the charges Monday, saying the alleged activity occurred primarily during 2023 and 2024. According to investigators, 13 of the defendants were Penn State students during the period under investigation, while the remaining defendant is the father of one of the accused students.

Authorities said at least four of the people charged remain Penn State students.

Investigators Identify Alleged Organizers

Prosecutors identified Agostino Abbatiello, 24, of Westbury, New York, and Thomas Robinson, 23, of Pittsburgh as alleged key suppliers in the operation.

Investigators accuse the pair of repeatedly traveling to Philadelphia and New York to obtain cocaine in larger quantities before bringing the drugs back to the State College area.

Authorities allege that the cocaine was subsequently divided, packaged and distributed, primarily to Penn State students. Investigators said some of the activity occurred at off-campus houses associated with Delta Upsilon and Sigma Chi.

The attorney general’s office said payments for the drugs were commonly made with cash or through payment applications.

Fraternity Pledges Allegedly Involved

One of the more serious allegations involves fraternity pledges. According to prosecutors, some pledges were allegedly instructed to help cut and package cocaine as part of their introduction into the organizations.

Sunday described the alleged operation as organized and profitable, saying investigators believe senior fraternity members, other fraternity members and pledges each played different roles.

Authorities emphasized that the alleged drug-packaging activity took place at off-campus fraternity locations rather than on Penn State’s main campus.

Charges Filed Against 14 People

Abbatiello, Robinson, Mohammed Hurabi and Lars Zeepvat are accused of felony offenses including corrupt organizations, conspiracy and dealing in proceeds of unlawful activity, along with related charges.

Robert Zanolla faces felony conspiracy and criminal use of a communication facility charges, as well as misdemeanor allegations.

Eight additional student-aged defendants—Liam Smith, Michael Brogno, Kaden Howe, Connor Hurleigh, Seth Beardsley, Kyle Ridpath, Daniel Price and Charles Wiseman—are facing misdemeanor charges involving alleged drug possession and drug paraphernalia.

Paul Robinson, identified as Thomas Robinson’s father, is the only defendant who was not a Penn State student. Investigators accuse him of helping conceal evidence, including allegedly keeping drugs and cash inside a safe. He also faces allegations related to hindering the investigation.

Authorities said most of the defendants facing felony charges were arraigned Monday. An arrest warrant was issued for Abbatiello, according to officials.

Penn State Takes Action Against Delta Upsilon

Penn State said it immediately placed Delta Upsilon on interim suspension following the announcement of the charges.

The university said its Office of Student Accountability and Conflict Response will conduct its own investigation into the allegations involving current students and the fraternity. Possible university disciplinary consequences can be separate from the criminal cases.

Penn State also noted that Sigma Chi is not a university-recognized organization and therefore operates outside the university’s formal support and oversight structure.

Delta Upsilon’s international organization said it was aware of the allegations. Executive Director Justin Kirk said individuals connected to the fraternity who were implicated had either been suspended or expelled, unless they had already resigned.

Investigation Focused on Alleged Distribution Network

Investigators said they used multiple investigative techniques to determine how cocaine was acquired, transported, prepared and distributed.

The alleged operation reportedly centered on social connections among students, fraternity members and acquaintances. Authorities said much of the alleged distribution occurred through personal contacts, fraternity-related gatherings and social events.

The Pennsylvania Attorney General’s Drug Strike Force Section is prosecuting the cases.

The allegations have not been proven in court. All defendants are presumed innocent unless and until they are found guilty.
