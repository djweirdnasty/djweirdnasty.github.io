Tim Curry, ‘Rocky Horror Picture Show’ and ‘It’ Icon, Dies at 80

Entertainment | Obituary

Published: Wednesday, August 26, 2026

Tim Curry, the acclaimed British actor whose unforgettable performances in “The Rocky Horror Picture Show,” “It,” “Clue” and “Home Alone 2: Lost in New York” made him one of the most recognizable performers of his generation, has died at age 80.

Curry died Tuesday night, Aug. 25, 2026, at his home in Toluca Lake, Los Angeles, according to his longtime manager and friend, Marcia Hurwitz. His representatives said he died peacefully.

His cause of death has not been publicly disclosed.

The Los Angeles Police Department confirmed that officers completed a death investigation involving an 80-year-old man at a Los Angeles residence Tuesday night, although authorities did not publicly identify Curry in connection with the report. TMZ reported that emergency personnel responded to Curry’s home late Tuesday, and no foul play is suspected.

A Career Defined by Unforgettable Characters

Curry’s career stretched across more than five decades and included film, television, theater and voice acting. Few performers demonstrated his ability to move between comedy, horror, musical theater and dramatic roles with such ease.

His defining breakthrough came in 1975 with “The Rocky Horror Picture Show,” in which he portrayed Dr. Frank-N-Furter, the flamboyant and eccentric scientist at the center of the cult musical.

The movie initially received a mixed response but eventually developed an enormous following through midnight screenings and audience participation. Curry’s performance became inseparable from the film’s enduring cultural legacy.

For generations of viewers, his portrayal of Frank-N-Furter became a symbol of theatrical freedom and individuality.

Curry later reflected on the character and the impact the role had on audiences, saying he was pleased that the character had given young people permission to embrace being different.

From Pennywise to Wadsworth

Curry demonstrated an entirely different side of his talent in the 1990 television adaptation of Stephen King’s “It.”

As Pennywise the clown, Curry transformed into one of television horror’s most recognizable villains. His unsettling performance helped make the character a lasting part of pop culture and introduced his work to another generation of fans.

He also showed his comedic abilities as Wadsworth, the mysterious butler in the 1985 movie “Clue.”

The film, based on the popular board game, eventually developed its own devoted following. Curry’s rapid-fire delivery and physical comedy helped make Wadsworth one of the movie’s most memorable characters.

His other major screen appearances included “Annie,” “The Hunt for Red October,” “The Three Musketeers,” “Muppet Treasure Island,” “Addams Family Reunion” and “Scary Movie 2.”

In “Home Alone 2: Lost in New York,” Curry played Mr. Hector, the suspicious and highly attentive concierge at the Plaza Hotel, adding another memorable supporting performance to his résumé.

A Star With Deep Theater Roots

Long before becoming a recognizable face in Hollywood, Curry established himself as a stage performer.

His professional theater career began in London, including an appearance in the musical “Hair.” It was through that production that he encountered Richard O’Brien, who would later write “The Rocky Horror Show,” the stage production that became the basis for the iconic movie.

Curry’s theater work remained an important part of his career even after he became internationally famous.

He received three Tony Award nominations, including recognition for his performances in “Amadeus,” “My Favorite Year” and “Spamalot.”

His performance as King Arthur in “Spamalot” earned him another Tony nomination in 2005.

Curry also received an Emmy Award for his voice work as Captain Hook on the animated series “Peter Pan and the Pirates.”

His Voice Reached Another Generation

Curry’s distinctive voice also allowed him to maintain a significant presence in animation and children’s entertainment.

He provided voices for productions including “The Wild Thornberrys,” “The Adventures of Jimmy Neutron, Boy Genius” and “Star Wars: The Clone Wars.”

These roles introduced Curry to audiences who may not have known him primarily for his work in “Rocky Horror” or “It.”

Life After His 2012 Stroke

Curry’s career was significantly affected by a major stroke in 2012.

The stroke left him partially paralyzed and unable to walk. He subsequently relied on a wheelchair and made fewer public appearances, although he continued working when possible.

Despite those physical limitations, Curry remained connected to acting and his fans.

In a 2025 appearance on “CBS Sunday Morning,” he discussed what continued to attract him to performing. He described acting as providing him with the freedom to become someone else.

His public appearances in later years offered glimpses of an actor who remained deeply connected to his craft even as his physical abilities changed.

Curry Reflected on His Legacy in His Memoir

In 2025, Curry released his memoir “Vagabond,” which arrived around the 50th anniversary of “The Rocky Horror Picture Show.”

The book gave Curry an opportunity to reflect on his long career and the difference between the characters audiences knew and the private person behind them.

Although audiences often associated him with eccentric, frightening or flamboyant characters, Curry emphasized that those roles did not define who he was personally.

That distinction was particularly important given the extraordinary range of characters he portrayed throughout his career.

Tributes Begin Following His Death

News of Curry’s death prompted tributes from former co-stars and organizations connected to his work.

Lesley Ann Warren, who appeared alongside Curry in “Clue,” remembered her longtime colleague as a brilliant and deeply funny performer.

Michael McKean, another “Clue” co-star, also paid tribute, recalling a final conversation centered on life, love and laughter.

Carol Burnett, who worked with Curry on “Annie,” remembered him as a performer uniquely capable of making villains both memorable and entertaining.

The official social media accounts associated with The Muppets also honored Curry’s performance as Long John Silver in “Muppet Treasure Island,” describing his participation in the production as a treasured part of the franchise’s history.

A Legacy That Crossed Generations

Curry’s unusual career allowed him to become several different icons to different generations.

To “Rocky Horror” fans, he was the theatrical Dr. Frank-N-Furter.

To horror audiences, he was Pennywise.

To comedy fans, he was Wadsworth.

To families, he was Mr. Hector in “Home Alone 2” and Long John Silver in “Muppet Treasure Island.”

And to theater audiences, he was an accomplished stage performer whose career earned multiple Tony nominations.

That range helped Curry remain relevant across generations rather than being defined by a single performance.

Curry never married and did not have children. He is survived by his nieces Kate Lewis, Julia and Patrick Bullman; great-nieces Josephine and Georgina; and nephew Mark Lewis.

For more than 50 years, Tim Curry gave audiences characters that were strange, frightening, hilarious, theatrical and unforgettable.

His death marks the passing of a performer whose voice, presence and unmistakable style left an enduring imprint on film, television, theater and popular culture.
