Bizarre Rules Confusion Causes 16-Minute Delay in Red Sox-Blue Jays Game

Sports | MLB

Published: Tuesday, August 11, 2026

A confusing sequence in the seventh inning caused a 16-minute delay Monday night during the Boston Red Sox’s 2-1 loss to the Toronto Blue Jays at Rogers Centre.

With runners on first and second and no outs, Boston’s Ceddanne Rafaela hit a fly ball to center field. Anthony Seigler, the runner at third, headed home and scored before Boston’s Nick Sogard was retired at first for what became the third out.

The problem was that Seigler appeared to leave third base without tagging up.

Toronto initially believed the inning was over and the run did not count. The umpires then gathered to discuss the play and contacted MLB’s replay center in New York for clarification.

After the rules check, crew chief Chris Guccione announced that Boston’s run would count because Seigler crossed home plate before the third out was recorded at first. Toronto had not appealed Seigler’s failure to tag up.

The ruling made the score 2-1, although Toronto held on for the victory.

The situation involved MLB Rule 5.09(c)(4), which covers appeal plays and potential “fourth out” situations.

Both teams appeared confused during the sequence, with several Blue Jays players already heading toward the dugout before the final out was completed.

Red Sox interim manager Chad Tracy later described the unusual inning as “strange” and “very weird.”
