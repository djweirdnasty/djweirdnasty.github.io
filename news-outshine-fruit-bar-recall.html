Check Your Freezer: Outshine Fruit Bars Pulled From Stores Over Possible Glass Contamination

National | Consumer

Published: Tuesday, August 18, 2026

A popular frozen snack is being pulled from store shelves after the manufacturer discovered a potential safety issue involving glass.

Dreyer's Grand Ice Cream has announced a voluntary recall affecting certain batches of Outshine Fruit Bars. The company said the products could contain foreign material, including glass.

The recall involves a limited number of products sold nationwide. According to the company, there have been no reported illnesses or injuries connected to the recalled items as of the announcement.

Which Outshine Bars Are Affected?

Consumers should pay particular attention to five flavors sold in 6-count boxes containing 2.5-ounce bars:

  • Strawberry
  • Watermelon
  • Grape
  • Tangerine
  • Black Cherry

The recall does not apply to every package of these flavors. Specific production batches are involved, so consumers should examine the bottom of their box for the corresponding UPC and batch information.

Some recalled products have best-before dates extending into 2027.

What Should You Do If You Have One?

If you discover a package that matches the recall information, do not eat the fruit bars. Consumers are being advised to throw the product away or take it back to the retailer for a refund.

Anyone who believes they may have consumed a product containing glass and is concerned about a possible injury should contact a healthcare professional.

Glass can cause cuts to the mouth and digestive tract, particularly when pieces have sharp edges. Symptoms following ingestion can vary depending on the size and location of the object.

Other Brands Not Included

Dreyer's said the recall is limited to the specified Outshine products. Other frozen-food brands produced by the company, including Häagen-Dazs, Drumstick, Edy's and Skinny Cow, are not part of this particular recall.

Consumers should check the batch code and UPC on the bottom of their Outshine package rather than assuming every product in the affected flavors is unsafe.

Distribution and Announcement

The recall was announced Aug. 16, 2026, and the affected products were distributed nationwide.
