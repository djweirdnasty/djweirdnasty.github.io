Tupac Shakur Murder Trial Begins Nearly 30 Years After Rapper's Death

Music News

Published: Monday, August 10, 2026

Nearly 30 years after Tupac Shakur was killed in Las Vegas, the case surrounding the legendary rapper's death is finally going before a jury.

Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, is charged with murder in connection with Shakur's September 1996 shooting. Prosecutors allege that Davis helped organize the attack and obtained the firearm that was used. Davis has pleaded not guilty.

Jury selection begins Monday, August 10, in Clark County District Court in Las Vegas. The trial is expected to last about a month and could bring renewed attention to one of the most closely watched unsolved killings in hip-hop history.

Shakur was riding in a BMW with Death Row Records co-founder Marion "Suge" Knight on September 7, 1996. The two were traveling toward Club 662 when a white Cadillac pulled alongside their vehicle. Gunfire erupted, striking Shakur multiple times. Knight was also wounded but survived.

Shakur was rushed to a Las Vegas hospital, where he died six days later at the age of 25.

The investigation remained unresolved for decades. Authorities eventually arrested Davis in 2023 after years of renewed attention surrounding his previous statements about the shooting.

Prosecutors are not necessarily claiming that Davis was the person who fired the fatal shots. Instead, they allege that he helped arrange the attack, obtained the gun and provided it to someone inside the Cadillac.

A major part of the prosecution's case is expected to involve Davis' own statements. He discussed the shooting in interviews and in his 2019 book, Compton Street Legend. He has also previously spoken with investigators about what happened that night.

Davis has since disputed portions of his earlier accounts, and his defense is expected to argue that some of his statements were exaggerated or unreliable. Prosecutors, however, are expected to point to details they say are supported by other evidence.

Authorities have previously identified Davis' nephew, Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, as a possible shooter. Anderson was never charged in Shakur's death and was killed in an unrelated shooting in Compton in 1998. Other people who were reportedly in the Cadillac that night have also died.

Prosecutors could call dozens of witnesses during the trial, including people connected to Shakur, Davis, Death Row Records and the gangs involved in the conflict surrounding the case.

Leonard Jefferson, who photographed Shakur shortly before the shooting, is expected to testify. The photograph he captured is considered the final known image of the rapper.

Former law enforcement officers, detectives and gang experts are also among the potential witnesses.

Knight could be another important figure in the proceedings because he was in the BMW with Shakur when the shooting occurred. Knight survived the attack but is currently serving a lengthy prison sentence in California for a separate fatal incident. He has previously indicated that he does not want to testify in Davis' case.

Sean "Diddy" Combs has also been mentioned in connection with the case because of allegations Davis previously made about the rivalry between Death Row Records and Bad Boy Records. Combs has denied any involvement in Shakur's killing. There is currently no indication that Combs will testify.

Tupac Shakur remains one of the most influential figures in hip-hop history. His death sparked decades of speculation about who was responsible for the shooting and whether others were involved in planning the attack.

The trial will now give prosecutors an opportunity to present their evidence against Davis and allow the defense to challenge those allegations.

If convicted, Davis could face life in prison.

The proceedings are expected to bring renewed focus to the events surrounding Shakur's final night and could provide jurors with the most detailed examination yet of the case that has remained a mystery for nearly three decades.
