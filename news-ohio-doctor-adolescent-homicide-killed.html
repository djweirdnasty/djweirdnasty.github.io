Ohio Doctor Who Studied Adolescent Homicide Killed in Alleged Plot Involving Her Daughter

National | Crime

Published: Sunday, August 23, 2026

An Ohio physician whose academic work examined the disturbing behavior of young people who commit homicide was fatally shot inside her Delaware County home, authorities say. Investigators now allege that the killing was part of a plan involving her 18-year-old daughter, the daughter’s boyfriend and his roommate.

Tamela D. Dutcher, 55, was killed shortly after midnight on August 14, 2026, after an armed confrontation unfolded inside the family’s Delaware-area residence. Her daughter, Bryanna Dutcher, 18, and two men identified as Christian Evans, 20, and Dajameous Payne, 21, have been charged in connection with the death.

The case has drawn particular attention because years before her death, Tamela Dutcher co-authored an academic paper examining juvenile homicide and the ways violent behavior can develop among adolescents.

Fatal shooting inside the family home

According to authorities and court documents cited by multiple outlets, deputies were called to the Dutcher residence shortly after midnight following a 911 call from Tamela’s husband.

Investigators allege that Tamela was sitting on a couch in the living room when Evans and Payne entered the home.

One shot was fired, striking Tamela in the face. Her husband reportedly rushed toward her after hearing the gunfire. Investigators say another shot was fired in his direction, but he was not struck.

The suspects then allegedly fled the house.

Tamela was transported to a hospital but died from her injuries.

The circumstances initially appeared to investigators to involve an unknown intruder. As detectives began examining the evidence and interviewing people connected to the family, however, attention turned toward individuals who allegedly had prior knowledge of the household.

Investigators allege daughter helped arrange the attack

Court documents describe an argument between Tamela and Bryanna earlier that day.

According to a probable-cause affidavit cited by ABC 6 and Oxygen, the disagreement involved household chores. Bryanna subsequently contacted Evans and discussed the confrontation.

Investigators allege that Evans responded by suggesting that he would have killed Tamela if he were in Bryanna’s position.

Authorities say Evans and Payne later went to the Dutcher residence.

Investigators allege that Bryanna provided information about where her parents were inside the home, allowing the men to determine when and where to enter.

According to the affidavit, messages recovered from the phones of the individuals involved included discussions about getting ready, putting on gloves and attacking both parents.

The messages are among the evidence prosecutors are expected to rely upon as the case moves forward.

Bryanna has denied involvement in planning the shooting, according to reports citing the investigation.

Police say initial story raised questions

After the shooting, investigators say Bryanna initially denied knowing who could have been responsible.

According to the affidavit, she told police she had not seen the shooter and suggested investigators consider a friend of her mother’s who allegedly owed her money.

Authorities later concluded that portions of Bryanna’s account were inconsistent with evidence they had gathered.

Police wrote that they believed she had repeatedly misled investigators about what she knew, potentially delaying efforts to identify the alleged shooters.

Bryanna was initially arrested on an obstruction-of-justice charge. Prosecutors later added a murder charge.

Evidence allegedly linked Evans and Payne to the shooting

Investigators eventually identified Evans and Payne as suspects and moved to arrest them.

According to court documents, police encountered the men near Evans’ apartment. Authorities allegedly observed Evans throwing a white garbage bag into a dumpster.

Inside the bag, investigators reportedly found a portion of a gun holster and two pairs of black pants.

Police subsequently stopped the men as they attempted to leave in Evans’ vehicle and arrested them.

Investigators also obtained information from Evans’ father, who reportedly told authorities that his son had sent him a message acknowledging that he had “f–ked up.”

Location information from a Life360 account allegedly placed Evans near the Dutcher residence around the time of the shooting.

Phone records also became an important part of the investigation.

Authorities say messages between Evans and Bryanna included references to what would happen after both parents died, as well as a suggestion that Bryanna obtain a gun.

The messages, according to investigators, were recovered after some had been deleted.

Alleged relationship was another focus of investigation

Investigators also examined the relationship between Bryanna and Evans.

According to the affidavit, Bryanna told authorities that her parents did not know she was dating Evans and that she believed they would force her out of the home if they discovered the relationship.

The affidavit characterizes Bryanna as describing her parents as “very racist.”

That allegation became part of investigators’ examination of the circumstances surrounding the shooting and the relationship between the teenager and Evans.

However, the exact role that the relationship and alleged family conflict played in the decision to target Tamela and her husband remains a matter for the criminal proceedings.

Dutcher had studied juvenile homicide

Perhaps the most striking element of the case is the connection between Tamela Dutcher’s professional work and the circumstances surrounding her death.

In 2002, Dutcher co-authored an academic paper with Daniel Davis titled The Violent and Homicidal Adolescent: Hearts Darkened Before Time. The article was published in the International Journal of Adolescent Medicine and Health.

The research examined violence and homicide committed by young people.

Among its observations was the finding that although most young homicide offenders are male, female juveniles who commit homicide are more likely to kill members of their own families.

That passage has taken on a disturbing significance following allegations that Dutcher was killed in a plot involving her own teenage daughter.

The paper also examined factors associated with juvenile violence and discussed potential interventions, including approaches involving creative activities and cognitive behavioral therapy.

Three defendants face serious charges

Bryanna Dutcher, Christian Evans and Dajameous Payne have all been charged in connection with Tamela Dutcher’s death.

The charges reported in court records include aggravated murder or murder-related offenses, with the precise charges differing in descriptions from various reports and filings. Bryanna also faces an obstruction-of-justice charge stemming from allegations that she misled investigators.

Prosecutors have accused the defendants of acting deliberately and with prior calculation.

All three have been held under court-ordered conditions that have included GPS monitoring, with reports indicating that bond was set at $300,000 each.

No conviction has been entered against any of the defendants, and the allegations remain to be tested in court.

Evans’ attorney questions his competency

The case took another turn when Evans’ attorney filed a suggestion of incompetency, asking that his client’s ability to participate meaningfully in the legal proceedings be evaluated.

Such a filing can lead to a court-ordered assessment of whether a defendant is mentally competent to understand the proceedings and assist in their own defense.

The filing does not determine whether Evans committed the crime. It concerns his ability to proceed through the criminal justice process.

Meanwhile, the prosecution’s case continues to center on the alleged communications between the defendants, evidence recovered after the shooting and investigators’ reconstruction of what happened inside the Dutcher home.

A case marked by an unsettling connection

The death of Tamela Dutcher has left investigators attempting to untangle an alleged family murder plot that prosecutors say began with a domestic disagreement and escalated into a planned attack.

The allegations are particularly unsettling because of Dutcher’s previous academic work examining the behavior of young homicide offenders.

Her 2002 research addressed the possibility of adolescents becoming involved in lethal violence and noted the unusual pattern of female juvenile offenders targeting relatives.

More than two decades later, authorities allege that Dutcher herself became the victim of a fatal attack involving her own daughter.

As the case proceeds, prosecutors will have to establish what each defendant allegedly knew, planned or did, while defense attorneys will have the opportunity to challenge the evidence and the state’s interpretation of the events.

For now, the investigation has transformed what initially appeared to be a deadly home invasion into a much broader alleged conspiracy involving a mother, her daughter and two young men — with the ultimate facts to be determined in court.
