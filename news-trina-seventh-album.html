Trina Teases Seventh Album as Potential Final Chapter of Her Rap Career

Music News

Published: Tuesday, August 18, 2026

After nearly three decades of breaking barriers in hip-hop, Trina may be preparing to close the book on her recording career.

The Miami rap veteran recently appeared on 99 JAMZ Live, where she revealed that she is considering making her upcoming seventh studio album her final full-length release. Trina said she still has “one more” album in her and pointed to the timing of the project as another reason to make it special.

Her last studio album, The One, arrived in 2019. If her next project comes out in 2026, it would mark seven years between albums and give her career discography a fitting conclusion with a seventh studio album.

From Miami Breakout to Rap Icon

Trina first became widely known in the late 1990s after appearing on Trick Daddy’s “Nann N***a.” Her performance helped establish her as one of Miami’s emerging rap voices and eventually led to a record deal with Slip-N-Slide Records and Atlantic Records.

She officially launched her solo career with Da Baddest B*h in 2000. The album introduced audiences to the bold personality and confident delivery that would become trademarks of Trina’s career.

Since then, she has remained one of the most recognizable women in Southern hip-hop. Her catalog includes six studio albums, numerous collaborations and years of performances that helped solidify her status as a Miami rap icon.

Recognition Beyond the Studio

Her accomplishments have continued well beyond the recording studio. In 2021, Trina faced off against fellow hip-hop legend Eve in a Verzuz battle. A year later, BET recognized her with its “I Am Hip Hop” award during the 2022 BET Hip Hop Awards.

Miami also honored the hometown star by naming a street after her earlier this year. The recognition, “Still The Baddest Street,” carried special meaning for Trina as she dedicated the honor to loved ones she has lost, including her brother Wilbrent Bain Jr., her mother Vernessa Taylor and her niece SUGA.

Philanthropy and Continued Presence

Trina’s influence also extends into philanthropy. Through the Diamond Doll Foundation, she has worked to assist young women and families. Her community efforts have included a back-to-school event in Miami offering students school supplies, entertainment, food and other resources.

Despite releasing no new studio album since 2019, Trina has remained active. She has continued performing and making appearances, including joining Cardi B during the Little Miss Drama Tour and sharing the stage with Rick Ross. She has also made an appearance on All Elite Wrestling’s Dynamite, showing that her influence reaches beyond traditional hip-hop spaces.

A Legacy Nearly Three Decades in the Making

Still, Trina’s comments suggest that she may be entering a different stage of her career. After years of recording, touring and maintaining her place in the culture, the Miami legend appears to be thinking about what comes next.

Whether the seventh album ultimately becomes her final project remains to be seen. But if Trina does decide to walk away from making full-length albums, she will be leaving behind a legacy built over almost 30 years—one that helped define the sound and image of Southern women in hip-hop.

For longtime fans, the possibility of a final Trina album makes her next chapter especially significant. Whatever she chooses, “Da Baddest” has already secured her place in rap history.
