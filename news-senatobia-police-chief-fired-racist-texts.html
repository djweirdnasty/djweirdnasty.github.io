Mississippi Police Chief Fired Amid Racist Text Scandal After 1-Year-Old Is Killed in Police Shooting

National

Published: Thursday, September 3, 2026

SENATOBIA, Miss. — A Mississippi police department is facing another leadership change after Police Chief Harold “Hal” Vanderford was fired amid allegations that he repeatedly used racist slurs in private text messages.

The Senatobia Board of Aldermen voted unanimously Tuesday to terminate Vanderford after he submitted a resignation that city officials declined to accept.

His dismissal comes as the community continues to deal with the June death of 1-year-old Kohen Wiley, a Black child who was fatally shot by a Senatobia police officer during an encounter outside a Walmart.

Racist Messages Put Chief Under Scrutiny

Vanderford’s position came under pressure after an investigation by The New York Times, Mississippi Today and Verite News examined private text conversations allegedly involving the police chief.

The reporting alleged that Vanderford repeatedly used racial slurs targeting Black people. Some of the messages reportedly dated back to before he became police chief and continued during his time leading the department.

The allegations prompted renewed concerns about the department’s leadership and internal culture.

City officials initially stood behind Vanderford. After meeting with him in August, officials said the chief had answered questions about the reported messages and would remain in his position.

That decision was later reversed when the board rejected his resignation and voted to fire him.

Toddler Was Killed During Police Response

The leadership controversy comes while investigators continue examining the circumstances surrounding Wiley’s death.

On June 14, officers responded to a reported shoplifting incident at a Walmart in Senatobia. Authorities said officers encountered a vehicle leaving the area and that one officer opened fire.

Wiley was struck and killed. An adult passenger was also seriously wounded.

The circumstances leading up to the shooting remain disputed.

Wiley’s family has said the boy was traveling with his mother and a family friend. His mother has denied that she or her companion stole anything from the store.

The officer who fired the shots has been placed on administrative leave while the Mississippi Bureau of Investigation handles the investigation.

Family Wants Answers and Video

Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who represents Wiley’s family, said Vanderford’s removal was appropriate but stressed that it does not resolve the family’s questions about the toddler’s death.

The family has been calling for the release of police body-camera and surveillance footage from the incident.

The Mississippi Bureau of Investigation has not publicly released the footage.

Crump has argued that the family deserves to see the recordings so they can better understand how the encounter unfolded.

Senatobia Department Has Faced Previous Controversies

Vanderford’s firing is the latest in a series of controversies involving the Senatobia Police Department.

The department has previously faced criticism over incidents involving Black residents, including the arrest of a 10-year-old Black child over public urination and a confrontation between an officer and a Black woman outside the Walmart where Wiley was later shot.

Those controversies contributed to the city’s decision in 2025 not to reappoint former Police Chief Richard Chandler.

Vanderford was subsequently selected to lead the department.

Now, less than a year after taking the position, he has been removed.

Interim Chief Takes Over

Lt. Angelica Maze will oversee the police department while city officials search for a permanent chief.

Some community members are calling for more than a change in leadership. They want an independent examination of how the department operates, including its handling of complaints, internal conduct and oversight.

Senatobia Alderman Chris McConnell has called for an independent investigation into the department’s leadership and practices.

Community activist Patrick Lumumba, who formed the Senatobia Committee for Accountability and Transparency following Wiley’s death, has also pushed for greater accountability.

For Wiley’s family, the central issue remains the unanswered questions surrounding the June 14 shooting.

The investigation remains ongoing, while the continued calls for video and greater transparency keep the Senatobia Police Department under intense scrutiny.
